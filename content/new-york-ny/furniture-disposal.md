# Furniture Disposal in NYC: How to Throw Out a Couch, Dresser or Bed Frame (2026)

**Quick answer:** The Department of Sanitation (DSNY) removes large furniture from the curb for free, with no appointment: up to 6 large items per collection day, wood and other non-recyclable pieces on your trash-only day, mostly metal or plastic pieces on your recycling day. If you can't get it down the stairs or out to the curb, a local junk removal company listed below can carry it out for a fee. Furniture that's still in good shape can be donated instead.

*Last verified November 26, 2026, against official New York City pages, linked in each section below.*

## Free: DSNY Curbside Large-Item Collection {#curbside}

- **What counts:** anything too large to fit in a bin or bag is a large (bulk) item. DSNY names bed frames, bookcases, couches and sofas, desks, dressers, and tables and chairs. ([DSNY: Furniture, Mattresses, and Rugs](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/furniture-mattresses.page))
- **Cost and booking:** free for NYC residents. Pick-up appointments are no longer offered and can't be scheduled; you just set items out on the right day. ([DSNY: Large Items](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/large-items.page))
- **Which day:** items that are mostly metal or rigid plastic go out with recycling. Everything else, including wood, fiberboard and particle board, goes out on your **trash-only day, not your recycling day**. Look up your days with the [DSNY collection schedule](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01801).
- **How many:** up to **6 large items** at the curb per collection day.
- **When to set out:** the evening before collection. DSNY's set-out rules say after 6 p.m. for bins (after 8 p.m. for bags placed directly on the curb at buildings with 10 or more units), and all waste should be out by midnight. Setting out at the wrong time or on the wrong day can bring a fine of $50, then $100, then $200 for repeat offenses. ([DSNY: Collection Laws for Residents](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/residents/collection-laws-residents.page))
- **Prep:** take furniture apart if you can, and bundle smaller pieces with tape or twine. Glass furniture, glass tabletops and mirrors can't be recycled: tape up large glass and double-bag anything broken, then set it out with trash.

**The catch:** you have to get the furniture down to the curb yourself, on the right night, and you can't put out more than 6 large items in one day.

## Drop It Off Yourself {#drop-off}

We found no City drop-off site for household furniture on DSNY's pages when we checked on November 26, 2026. DSNY's route for furniture is free curbside collection. Its Special Waste Drop-Off sites take items such as harmful household products, not furniture.

If the piece is still usable, the City's [donateNYC](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/donate/site/) program lists local nonprofits and reuse groups that take donated goods, so you can look for one that accepts furniture near you. Habitat NYC and Westchester ReStore, listed above, offers donation pickup across the boroughs. Call ahead: donation groups usually only take clean furniture in good condition.

## Special Cases {#special}

- **Mattresses and box springs:** they go out on your trash-only day like other furniture, but must be fully sealed in a plastic bag to stop bed bugs spreading. DSNY lists fines of $50, $100 and $200 for repeat violations ([DSNY: Collection Laws for Residents](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/residents/collection-laws-residents.page)), and its furniture page says fines can reach $300.
- **Rugs and carpets:** roll them up, tie them securely with twine and set them out with trash.
- **Exercise equipment:** treadmills, ellipticals and stationary bikes follow the same large-item rules. Take them apart if possible and remove any batteries first.
- **Renovation debris:** furniture-sized items from a small DIY project (cabinets, sinks, tubs) can go out as large items, but waste from a professional contractor can't. For a major renovation, arrange a dumpster through a private hauler. ([DSNY: Construction Debris](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/collection/get-rid-of/construction-debris.page))
- **Fridges, freezers and air conditioners** have their own special disposal rules: see [NYC appliance rules](/new-york-ny/#appliances).

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

### Does NYC pick up furniture for free?

Yes. DSNY collects couches, dressers, tables, bed frames and other large furniture at the curb at no charge to residents.

### Do I need to schedule a bulk pickup for furniture in NYC?

No. DSNY stopped taking pick-up appointments for large items. Put the furniture out the evening before the correct collection day and crews take it.

### What day do I put furniture out in NYC?

Your trash-only day for wood, fiberboard, upholstered and other non-recyclable pieces. A piece that's mostly metal or rigid plastic, like a metal bed frame or plastic patio chairs, goes out on your recycling day instead.

### How many pieces of furniture can I put out at once?

Six large items per collection day. If you're clearing out a whole apartment, spread it over several collection days or hire a junk removal company.

### Can I put a mattress out with my old furniture?

Yes, on your trash-only day, but only if it's sealed completely in a plastic bag. An unbagged mattress can be left behind and get you a fine.
