# Christmas Tree Disposal by City

After the holidays, most US cities will take a real Christmas tree for a few weeks and turn it into mulch or compost. The details are local: some cities collect at the curb on your regular day, some open drop-off corrals in parks, and some only take a tree if it is cut to fit an organics cart. A few cities have already posted 2026-27 dates. Many have not, and for those we show the latest published season and label it clearly.

This page was checked on November 10, 2026. Rules, hours, sites and fees change. Confirm on the official page linked in your city's section before you set a tree out or drive anywhere.

## Your Options

1. **Curbside collection.** In some cities this is a standing yard-waste or compost rule. In others it is a short special window after Christmas. Follow that city's set-out day, size limit and placement rules.
2. **Drop-off and mulching sites.** Parks, sanitation yards, transfer stations and "treecycling" events take bare trees for chipping. Hours and site lists change every year.
3. **Nonprofit or Scout pickup.** Some official city pages list fundraising pickups or community chipper events. We only name groups that appear on an official page we checked, and we do not invent dates for them.
4. **Paid junk removal.** If you missed the city's window, the tree is too large, or you cannot get it to the curb, a local junk removal company may take it. HowToDumpIt does not haul anything, and we do not post prices here. The exclusive Recommended junk removal spot on a city page, when one is listed, can offer tree pickup.

## How to Prepare the Tree

These are the rules that show up again and again on official pages. Your city may be stricter, so use the city section below.

- Remove lights, ornaments, tinsel, garland, nails, wire, plastic bags, netting and the stand (including wooden stands, where the city says so).
- Do not bag the tree unless your city says otherwise. Many compost programs will leave a bagged tree.
- Flocked (fake-snow) and painted trees are refused by many mulching programs. A few cities accept flocked trees or tell you to put them in the trash. Follow the local rule.
- Artificial trees are usually trash or bulky collection, not mulch. Some cities say to recycle a metal pole and base separately.
- Watch size limits. Common official instructions are to cut the tree in half if it is taller than 6 or 8 feet, or to cut it into 3-foot or 4-foot pieces so it fits in a cart with the lid closed.
- If you miss the window, look for a green-bin or cart fallback, a year-round transfer station (fees may apply), or a junk removal company listed on your city guide.
