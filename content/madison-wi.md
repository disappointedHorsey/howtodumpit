# Junk Disposal in Madison, WI: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in **Madison**, item by item. The City of Madison Streets Division picks up **large items at the curb when you schedule them** with an online work order, and it runs **three drop-off sites** for residents. Most large items have no fee; a short list of appliances and tires does, but only for homes whose recycling isn't collected by the City. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Questions? Call Streets East at **608-246-4532** or Streets West at **608-266-4681**.

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Mattresses are one of the City's standard large items. Fill out a **large item work order**, choose your set-out Sunday, and put the mattress at the curb that day; crews collect it sometime during the following work week. Mattresses are not on the City's list of items with a fee. Or take it to one of the three Streets drop-off sites. ([City of Madison: Large Items and Appliances](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/large-items-and-appliances); [Items with Fees](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/large-items-and-appliances/fees-for-items/items-with))

### Step by Step: City Large Item Collection

1. **Fill out the work order first.** List each item and pick a set-out Sunday. You won't get a sticker; the work order is your booking.
2. **Set items out on that Sunday only.** Crews won't come early, and if you put items out late you may miss the pickup.
3. **Place them right.** Items must be less than **8 feet** long, kept away from carts and brush or yard waste piles, **4 feet** from trees, cars, poles, mailboxes and hydrants, and out of the street and sidewalk.
4. **Separate metal from the rest.** Put appliances and metal items in a different pile from furniture and lumber; different crews collect them.
5. **Allow for delays.** Holidays, fall leaf collection, winter storms and severe weather can delay large item collection.

**Bed bugs:** We did not find a City rule for wrapping bed-bug or soiled mattresses. Ask the Streets Division before you set one out.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Couches, chairs, tables and other furniture** go out through the same **large item work order**, with no fee. Bigger buildings are the exception: since January 1, 2025, the Streets Division doesn't provide large item collection for properties with **more than 8 units**, including apartments and condominiums. ([City of Madison: No Large Item Collection for Properties with More than 8 Units](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/large-items-and-appliances/no-large-item-collection-for))

**Drop-off sites:** Madison residents and taxpayers can bring large items to the **East** (4602 Sycamore Ave.), **West** (402 South Point Rd.) or **Central** (1504 Quann-Olin Parkway) drop-off site. All three keep the same hours: through **December 3, 2026**, Monday and Friday 7:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m., Tuesday and Thursday 7:30 a.m. to 7 p.m., and Saturday 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.; from **December 4, 2026, to April 9, 2027**, Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 7:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. (closed Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday). Sites close on City holidays. ([City of Madison: Drop-Off Sites](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/drop-off-sites))

**Drop-off rules:** bring proof of residency or taxpayer status (a driver's license or a utility or tax bill works). You get **one trip a day**, up to what fits in a standard pickup bed or a 5-by-8-foot trailer, and you unload it yourself. Contractors can't use the sites. ([Rules & Restrictions to the Drop-Off Sites](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/drop-off-sites/rules-restrictions-to-the-drop-off-sites))

**Apartments and condos over 8 units:** ask your property manager; if the building uses metal dumpsters, its private hauler will collect large items under its own rules and charges. You can also use a City drop-off site or the Dane County Landfill.

### Outside City Limits?

The Streets Division serves the City of Madison only, and its drop-off sites need proof that you live in or own property in the City. Elsewhere in **Dane County**, the **Dane County Landfill** (7102 Maahic Way; Monday to Friday 6:30 a.m. to 3:15 p.m., Saturday 8 a.m. to 10:45 a.m.) takes larger loads and is open Saturdays in winter. ([Dane County Department of Waste & Renewables: Location and Hours](https://landfill.danecounty.gov/about/location)) Neighboring cities and towns run their own collection, so check your own municipality's page. Apartment, condo and HOA communities with a **private hauler** should ask their property manager or HOA.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, washers, dryers, stoves and water heaters** go out with a large item work order or to a drop-off site. Set them apart from non-metal items, and remove doors from refrigerators and freezers. ([Large Items and Appliances](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/large-items-and-appliances))

**Who pays a fee:** if the City collects your green recycling cart, you pay **no fee**; it's covered by the Resource Recovery Special Charge on your Municipal Services bill. If a private company collects your recycling, you pay **$35** per large appliance (refrigerators over 50 pounds, freezers, AC units, dehumidifiers, washers, dryers, dishwashers, stoves, water heaters and more) and **$15** for dorm-size fridges and microwaves, through the work order or with a fee sticker bought before you go to a drop-off site. ([City of Madison: Items with Fees](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/large-items-and-appliances/fees-for-items/items-with))

**Freon / refrigerants:** Never cut the lines or release refrigerant yourself; the US EPA says the final disposer in the disposal chain is responsible for making sure refrigerant is recovered. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**TVs, computers, monitors, printers, phones and most things with a plug** go to a **Streets Division drop-off site**, with **no fee** for City of Madison residents and property owners. Don't put them in your carts or at the curb: City staff may remove a TV or computer left at the curb and charge a **$30** fee. Businesses can't use the City's electronics recycling. Holiday and string lights are accepted at the drop-off sites too. ([City of Madison: TVs, Computers, and Electronics Recycling](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/how-do-i-dispose-of/electronics)) Wipe personal data before you let a device go.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**Dane County Clean Sweep** (7020 Maahic Way, the second Maahic Way entrance marked "Clean Sweep") takes oil-based paint, stains, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, automotive fluids and other household chemicals. It's open **Monday to Friday, 7:15 a.m. to 3:15 p.m.**, and **Saturday, 8 a.m. to 10:45 a.m.** Dane County households pay **$15 per trip** for hazardous waste (out-of-county households pay $75) and must show proof of residency. **Latex paint** isn't hazardous: Clean Sweep says to dry it out and put it in your regular garbage. Call 608-838-3212 with questions. ([Dane County Clean Sweep](https://landfill.danecounty.gov/Dane-County-Clean-Sweep))

**Holiday closures:** Clean Sweep and the landfill are closed Christmas Eve and Christmas Day (December 24-25) and New Year's Eve and New Year's Day (December 31-January 1). ([Location and Hours](https://landfill.danecounty.gov/about/location))

## Christmas Trees {#christmas-trees}

**Christmas tree short answer:** The Streets Division runs **two rounds of curbside Christmas tree collection** in January, and you can also bring a tree to a City drop-off site. Remove the stand and every decoration, don't bag the tree, and set it on the terrace by the street at least 4 feet from obstructions. Wreaths, garlands and boughs go in the trash. The 2026-27 dates were **not posted** as of November 25, 2026.

**Last season (2025-26):** trees set out by **6:30 a.m. on January 5, 2026**, were collected over roughly January 5-16, and trees set out by **6:30 a.m. on January 20, 2026**, over roughly January 20-30. Trees put out after the second round waited until spring brush collection. Snowstorms can pause tree collection, because the same crews drive the plows. ([City of Madison: How to Have Your Christmas Tree Collected](https://www.cityofmadison.com/news/2025-12-26/how-to-have-your-christmas-tree-collected))

**Holiday collection:** the City is closed on Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year's Eve and New Year's Day, and drop-off sites are closed those days. For these double holidays, the City says a pickup that falls on the first holiday moves one day earlier and a pickup that falls on the second holiday moves to the next work day after the break; your own collection calendar shows the exact day. ([City of Madison: Holiday Schedules](https://www.cityofmadison.com/streets/trash-recycling/guidelines/holiday-schedules))

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

### Does Madison pick up large items?

Yes. Fill out a large item work order, choose a set-out Sunday, and the Streets Division collects the items the following work week. Properties with more than 8 units aren't eligible.

### Is Madison large item pickup free?

For most items, yes. Fees apply only to some appliances, tires and small engines, and only for homes whose recycling isn't collected by the City.

### Where do I take a TV in Madison, WI?

To a Streets Division drop-off site, free for City residents. Don't leave it at the curb; the City may charge a $30 fee.

### Where can I get rid of old paint in Madison?

Dry out latex paint and put it in the trash. Take oil-based paint and chemicals to Dane County Clean Sweep, $15 per trip for Dane County households.
