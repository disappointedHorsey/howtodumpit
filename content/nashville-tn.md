# Junk Disposal in Nashville, TN: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Nashville and Davidson County, item by item. Metro Nashville doesn't collect bulky items in your curbside cart, so most options are a drop-off at a Metro **Convenience Center** or a curbside removal request through the **Davidson County Sheriff's Office**. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page, use hubNashville, or call 311 (615-862-5000) before you load up the car.

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Convenience Center drop-off at East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro ($12 per mattress or box spring, up to 4 per month), or Sheriff's Office curbside bulk removal |
| Couch / furniture | One bulk item free per visit at any Convenience Center (fees for more), or Sheriff's Office curbside bulk removal |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Refrigerators are on the free scrap-metal list at Convenience Centers; for window or wall AC units, call Metro first to confirm where to take them |
| TV / computer | Free e-waste drop-off at East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro (up to 100 pounds per day, 1 TV up to 80 inches); e-waste is banned from the trash |
| Paint / chemicals | Oil-based paint and household chemicals free at East or Ezell Pike (up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds); latex paint is not accepted, so dry it out and trash it |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Take it to the **East, Ezell Pike or Omohundro Convenience Center**. It costs **$12 per mattress or box spring**, with a limit of **4 per month** per household. Mattresses are **not** covered by the free daily bulk item. Or, if you live in a house on a public road, request a free curbside pickup from the **Davidson County Sheriff's Office Bulk Item Removal** program.

<!--LISTINGS-->

### Step by Step: Convenience Center Drop-Off

1. Check whether it's still usable. Metro suggests donating usable furniture to secondhand stores or listing it online before sending it to the landfill.
2. Pick a center that takes mattresses: **East** (943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr, Nashville, TN 37207), **Ezell Pike** (3254 Ezell Pk, Nashville, TN 37211) or **Omohundro** (1019 Omohundro Pl, Nashville, TN 37210). The Anderson Lane center in Madison does not take mattresses. ([Hours and locations](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers/hours-and-locations))
3. Go **Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** Centers are closed on Metro holidays.
4. Bring a **Tennessee driver's license showing a Davidson County address**. You unload your own items.
5. Pay **$12 per piece**. Cash (not accepted at Omohundro), check and credit or debit cards are accepted. Cards carry a **2.8% fee** with a **$1.95 minimum**.

### Step by Step: Sheriff's Office Curbside Bulk Removal

1. Check that you qualify. The Sheriff's Office removes items from the **front curb only**. It can't come onto private property such as apartment, condo or townhome complexes, private roads, gated communities or business properties. Apartment and condo residents are asked to place items at the front curb by the complex's main entrance. Items left from an eviction can't be removed.
2. Call to make a request (the request must come from the homeowner or tenant). You'll be asked for the items, the service address, a phone number and the resident's name. See the [Sheriff's Office page](https://sheriff.nashville.gov/bulk-item-removal/) for how to reach the program; we didn't find a phone number on that page.
3. Put items at the curb **by 6:30 a.m.** on the date you're given. The office has **up to seven business days** from that date to remove them.
4. Don't bag anything. "Nothing in bags" is on the not-accepted list, along with glass or mirrored items, paint cans, tires, chemicals and drywall.

### What Not to Do

- **Don't** put a mattress in your curbside cart or leave it by the cart. Metro says items too bulky for a cart go to a Convenience Center.
- **Don't** haul it in a commercial vehicle or one with business advertising; Convenience Centers turn those away.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

Every Convenience Center (East, Ezell Pike, Omohundro and Anderson Lane at 939A Anderson Ln, Madison) accepts **3 bags of trash plus 1 bulk item free** once per day. Couches, chairs, sofas, dressers, mirrors, tables, toilets, vehicle seats and windows are on Metro's bulk disposal list. Extra items are charged by volume: **$10** for less than 1 cubic yard (no higher than the truck bed, up to 2 visits a day), or **$20 per cubic yard** for 1 to 8 cubic yards (no trailers longer than 12 feet; no cargo vans or trailers at Anderson Lane). ([Convenience Centers](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers), [Bulky Item Collection](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers/bulky-item-collection))

The **Sheriff's Office Bulk Item Removal** program also takes old furniture, sofas and couches, lawn and baby furniture, carpets and rugs, and exercise equipment from the front curb (see the mattress section above for the rules). ([Sheriff's Office](https://sheriff.nashville.gov/bulk-item-removal/))

Small amounts of do-it-yourself construction and demolition material go to **Omohundro only** for $20. The fee table says up to 2 cubic yards, while the Bulky Item Collection page says residents are limited to 1 cubic yard, so confirm before you load up. Convenience Centers don't take brick, concrete, dirt, rock, roofing shingles, siding, pallets, asbestos or several other materials listed on the city page. For larger loads, Metro lists private sites (American Farms, Triune Residuals Management and Middle Tennessee Environmental Center); contact each one for fees.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

Metro's [Bulky Item Collection page](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers/bulky-item-collection) lists **refrigerators**, dishwashers, ovens and ranges, washers and dryers, and other large household appliances as scrap metal. Residents can drop off **one bulk scrap metal item** (or up to three trash bags of scrap metal) **at no charge** at a Convenience Center.

**AC units:** Metro's [Waste Management Policies document](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2025-10/Solid_Waste_Policies.pdf) says A/C units must be taken to the **East Center** for the Freon to be removed, with a limit of **1 A/C unit per month**. That document doesn't match the current web pages on every limit, so call Metro Waste Services at 615-862-5000 (or 311) to confirm before you bring an AC unit or any appliance with Freon.

The Sheriff's Office Bulk Item Removal program also lists refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, water heaters, ranges, washers and dryers, and microwaves among the items it removes from the curb. ([Sheriff's Office](https://sheriff.nashville.gov/bulk-item-removal/))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

Electronic waste is **banned from Metro trash containers** under Metro Code 10.20.095. Residents with valid Davidson County ID can recycle it **free** at the **East, Ezell Pike and Omohundro** Convenience Centers (not Anderson Lane), Tuesday to Saturday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. ([Electronic Waste Recycling](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers/electronic-waste-recycling))

- You can bring up to **100 pounds** of household e-waste per day, including **1 TV** (no wider than **80 inches**), **1 computer system** (computer plus monitor; one per month), 1 microwave or other kitchen appliance, 1 printer, copier or scanner, plus phones, tablets, cables, keyboards and other small electronics.
- **Not accepted:** electronics from a business, truck or trailer loads, e-waste in commercial or work vehicles, or e-waste from out-of-county residents. Businesses should contact a private e-waste recycler.
- The Sheriff's Office curbside program also lists televisions, computers and monitors.

Wipe personal data from phones and computers before you drop them off.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

Household hazardous waste is accepted **free** at the **East** (943A Doctor Richard G. Adams Dr) and **Ezell Pike** (3254 Ezell Pk) Convenience Centers, **up to 15 gallons or 100 pounds** per household per month. Accepted items include **oil-based paint**, paint thinner, stain and varnish, adhesives and caulk, antifreeze, motor oil, brake and transmission fluid, gasoline, car and rechargeable batteries, fluorescent tubes and CFL bulbs, mercury thermometers, pesticides, weed killer, pool chemicals and household cleaners. Cooking grease is also taken at Omohundro. **Not accepted:** latex or acrylic paint, water-based stains, propane tanks, ammunition, explosives, medical waste, unused medications, and business waste. Pack containers in sturdy boxes lined with newspaper, keep them in their original containers, and don't mix liquids. ([Household Hazardous Waste Collection](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/convenience-centers/household-hazardous-waste))

**Latex paint:** Metro doesn't take latex (water-based) paint at its hazardous waste sites. Use it up or give it away. If a can has **one inch or less** left, take the lid off and let it dry in a well-ventilated area away from kids and pets. If it has **more than an inch**, stir in shredded paper, sawdust, mulch, kitty litter or a paint hardener until it's dry. Then put the can, in a trash bag, in your regular trash. Never pour paint into a storm drain, sink or toilet. ([Metro latex paint disposal guide (PDF)](https://www.nashville.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/Latex-Paint-Disposal-Policy.pdf))

Not sure where something goes? Metro's Waste Wizard in [Nashville's Waste and Recycling App](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/waste-services/trash-and-recycling-services/recycling-app) looks up items, or call 615-924-2633.
