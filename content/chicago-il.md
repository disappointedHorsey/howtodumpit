# Junk disposal in Chicago, IL: mattresses, couches, furniture, appliances, TVs & electronics, paint & hazardous waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Chicago, item by item. Every option links to the official city or county page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call your city's service line.

## Quick answer by item

| Item | Best verified option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Free DSS bulk pickup (arrange via 311 / ward office) |
| Couch / furniture | Free DSS bulk pickup for 1–4 unit homes (arrange first) |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | DSS bulk pickup for fridges/freezers (remove doors; arrange first); HCCRF does not take appliances or freon |
| TV / computer | Free drop-off at the City's HCCRF, 1150 N. North Branch St |
| Paint / chemicals | HCCRF takes oil-based paint and chemicals; latex paint not accepted (dry it out) |

## Mattresses and box springs {#mattresses}


**Mattress short answer:** If you live in a **single-family home or a building with four units or fewer** served by Chicago Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS), bulk waste collection — including oversized household items — is covered with **no extra fee** beyond the residential garbage fee. **Arrange pickup first** with your ward/district sanitation office or through **311** before you put the mattress in the alley or at the curb. If the mattress is clean and pest-free, you can also donate it at the **Chicago Furniture Bank** (drop-off on S Whipple St).

<!--LISTINGS-->

### Step by step: city bulk pickup

1. Confirm you get **city (DSS) garbage service**. DSS serves single-family homes and apartment buildings of **four units or less**. Buildings with **five or more units** must use a private hauler — ask your landlord or property manager how bulky items are handled.
2. **Call 311** (or use 311.chicago.gov) **or contact your ward/district Streets and Sanitation office** and tell them you have a mattress (or other bulk item) for pickup. Find district office phones on the city's Division and Sanitation Offices page.
3. Wait for instructions. City guidance is to arrange the pick-up **before** putting any large item out; wards have ticketed residents who set bulk out without arranging it.
4. On the day/side they tell you, place the mattress where your trash is normally collected (many blocks use the **alley**; some use the curb). Do **not** block the alley, sidewalk, or street.
5. If the mattress has **bed bugs**, Chicago municipal code requires it to be **totally enclosed in a plastic bag and labeled as bed-bug infested** before it goes out for trash pickup. Bed-bug-infested bedding must not be recycled.

### What not to do / what the city won't take this way

- **Don't** put a mattress in the Blue Cart — mattresses are not a Blue Cart recyclable.
- **Don't** leave a mattress on the public way without arranging collection (illegal dumping / fly dumping risk, and a sanitation-code ticket risk).
- **Building/remodeling debris** is your responsibility to remove; city residential collection is not for contractor waste.
- Exact item lists and set-out quirks can vary by ward. Some ward offices publish different day-of-set-out advice than the citywide "arrange first" pages — **confirm with 311** for your address. *(Specific size/quantity limits for mattresses were not published on the city pages checked for this guide.)*

### Live in a 5+ unit building, suburb, or Cook County town?

City DSS bulk collection does not apply. Use your building's private scavenger contract, or check your suburb/township waste page. *(Suburb-by-suburb section: to be researched.)*

### Need it gone today / can't move it yourself?

City timing depends on your ward's workload. If you need same-day haul-away from inside the home, a paid junk removal service is the fallback. *(Paid options will be added once they're verified.)*

If the mattress is still usable, Chicago Furniture Bank drop-off or their scheduled pickup (suggested donation) is the donation path we could verify.

### Reporting illegal dumping

Report fly dumping (including dumped mattresses) through **311**. Illegal dumping endangers public health and can attract pests.

*Sources: City of Chicago Garbage Fee FAQ; Sanitation Code Violation / DSS guidance; Division and Sanitation Offices; Municipal Code §7-28-370 (bed-bug disposal); Chicago Furniture Bank donation page — checked 2026-10-11. Rules change, so confirm with 311 before you set anything out.*

## Couches and furniture {#furniture}

DSS bulk collection covers **oversized household items** for single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer, at no extra fee beyond the residential garbage fee. Follow the same steps as the mattress section: **arrange pickup first through 311 or your ward/district sanitation office**, then set it out where and when they tell you. Buildings with 5+ units use a private hauler. ([City garbage fee FAQ](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/garbage-fee-faq.html))

## Appliances (fridges, freezers, AC: freon) {#appliances}

**Fridges and freezers:** City sanitation guidance says to **always remove doors or lids** from discarded refrigerators and freezers, and to **arrange a bulk pick-up with your Streets and Sanitation ward superintendent before putting any large item out**. Start with **311** or your ward/district sanitation office (same path as mattresses and furniture). This applies to DSS-served single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer. ([Sanitation Code Violation / DSS guidance](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/streets/provdrs/streets_san/svcs/sanitation_ordinance.html))

**What HCCRF will not take:** The Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility **does not accept** household appliances (including refrigerators, washers, dryers, stoves), air conditioners, HVAC equipment, or refrigerants/freons. Do not take freon appliances there. ([HCCRF accepted/not-accepted list](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

**Window AC and other freon units:** The city pages we checked spell out door-removal and bulk-pickup rules for refrigerators and freezers. They do not publish a separate, confirmed process for window air conditioners. **Ask 311** whether your address can put a window AC out on a bulk pickup, and what prep they require.

## TVs, computers and electronics (e-waste) {#electronics}

Drop off computers, monitors, TVs, printers, keyboards, game consoles, DVD players, cable boxes and similar devices at the **Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF), 1150 N. North Branch St.**, open **Tuesday 7 a.m.–12 p.m., Thursday 2–7 p.m., and the first Saturday of each month 8 a.m.–3 p.m.** Residential only. Only listed items are accepted, and the City notes that once the truck is full, no more electronics are taken. Don't leave items outside after hours. ([Chicago Recycles: E-Waste](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/chicago-recycles/home/other-wastes.html); [HCCRF](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

## Paint and household hazardous waste {#hazardous}

The **HCCRF** (same address and hours as above) accepts household chemicals, **oil-based paint, aerosol paint and thinners**, solvents, auto fluids, pesticides, CFLs, many batteries, BBQ propane tanks, and mercury items. It does **not** accept **latex, acrylic or water-based paint**, alkaline batteries, explosives, medical waste, or pharmaceuticals. For latex paint, the City says to use it up, donate it, or **dry it out** (kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust) and put it in the trash. ([HCCRF](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

*Rules, hours and fees change. Confirm on the linked official page before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
