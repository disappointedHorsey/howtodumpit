# Chicago Bulk Item Pickup (Bulky Trash): Mattresses, Furniture, Appliances, TVs and Paint

**Chicago bulk item pickup in one line:** if the Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS) collects your garbage (single-family homes and buildings of four units or fewer), bulky items such as couches, mattresses and fridges are picked up at **no extra cost** beyond the City garbage fee, but you must **arrange the pickup first** through **311** or your ward's Streets and Sanitation superintendent before you put anything out. Buildings with five or more units use a private hauler. **TVs, computers and oil-based paint** go to the City's free **Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF)**, and latex paint is dried out and trashed. Below: item-by-item rules, then answers to common Chicago bulk pickup questions. Every option links to the official City or county page we checked, and where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call 311 before you set anything out.

## Quick Answer by Item

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}


**Mattress short answer:** If you live in a **single-family home or a building with four units or fewer** served by Chicago Department of Streets and Sanitation (DSS), bulk waste collection — including oversized household items — is covered with **no extra fee** beyond the residential garbage fee. **Arrange pickup first** with your ward/district sanitation office or through **311** before you put the mattress in the alley or at the curb. If the mattress is clean and pest-free, you can also donate it at the **Chicago Furniture Bank** (drop-off on S Whipple St).

<!--LISTINGS-->

### Step by Step: City Bulk Pickup

1. Confirm you get **city (DSS) garbage service**. DSS serves single-family homes and apartment buildings of **four units or less**. Buildings with **five or more units** must use a private hauler — ask your landlord or property manager how bulky items are handled.
2. **Call 311** (or use 311.chicago.gov) **or contact your ward/district Streets and Sanitation office** and tell them you have a mattress (or other bulk item) for pickup. Find district office phones on the city's Division and Sanitation Offices page.
3. Wait for instructions. City guidance is to arrange the pick-up **before** putting any large item out; wards have ticketed residents who set bulk out without arranging it.
4. On the day/side they tell you, place the mattress where your trash is normally collected (many blocks use the **alley**; some use the curb). Do **not** block the alley, sidewalk, or street.
5. If the mattress has **bed bugs**, Chicago municipal code requires it to be **totally enclosed in a plastic bag and labeled as bed-bug infested** before it goes out for trash pickup. Bed-bug-infested bedding must not be recycled.

### What Not to Do / What the City Won't Take This Way

- **Don't** put a mattress in the Blue Cart — mattresses are not a Blue Cart recyclable.
- **Don't** leave a mattress on the public way without arranging collection (illegal dumping / fly dumping risk, and a sanitation-code ticket risk).
- **Building/remodeling debris** is your responsibility to remove; city residential collection is not for contractor waste.
- Exact item lists and set-out quirks can vary by ward. Some ward offices publish different day-of-set-out advice than the citywide "arrange first" pages — **confirm with 311** for your address. *(Specific size/quantity limits for mattresses were not published on the city pages checked for this guide.)*

### Need It Gone Today / Can't Move It Yourself?

City timing depends on your ward's workload. If you can't wait or need it carried out of the home, use one of the local junk removal companies listed above.

If the mattress is still usable, Chicago Furniture Bank drop-off or their scheduled pickup (suggested donation) is the donation path we could verify.

### Reporting Illegal Dumping

Report fly dumping (including dumped mattresses) through **311**. Illegal dumping endangers public health and can attract pests.

*Sources: City of Chicago Garbage Fee FAQ; Sanitation Code Violation / DSS guidance; Division and Sanitation Offices; Municipal Code §7-28-370 (bed-bug disposal); Chicago Furniture Bank donation page — checked October 11, 2026. Rules change, so confirm with 311 before you set anything out.*

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

DSS bulk collection covers **oversized household items** for single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer, at no extra fee beyond the residential garbage fee. Follow the same steps as the mattress section: **arrange pickup first through 311 or your ward/district sanitation office**, then set it out where and when they tell you. Buildings with 5+ units use a private hauler. ([City garbage fee FAQ](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/garbage-fee-faq.html))

### Outside City Limits?

City bulk collection only covers Chicago addresses that the Department of Streets and Sanitation serves: single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer. Suburban cities and villages in Cook County run their own trash service through their public works departments, usually with a contracted hauler, so check your own village or city's public works page. Cook County itself doesn't manage hauling contracts or services for villages, cities or townships. ([Cook County: Solid Waste Management Plan 2024-2029](https://www.cookcountyil.gov/sites/g/files/ywwepo161/files/documents/2024-07/Solid%20Waste%20Plan_FINAL_7.25.24.pdf)) In unincorporated Cook County, service varies: a few townships have contracts for residents, some municipalities serve adjoining unincorporated areas, and elsewhere residents arrange their own collection. ([Cook County: Solid Waste Management Plan 2018 Update](https://www.cookcountyil.gov/sites/g/files/ywwepo161/files/service/solid-waste-management-plan-2018-update-final.pdf)) If you live in a building with 5 or more units or an HOA with a private hauler, ask your building manager or hauler how bulky items are handled. *(Checked October 31, 2026.)*

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Fridges and freezers:** City sanitation guidance says to **always remove doors or lids** from discarded refrigerators and freezers, and to **arrange a bulk pick-up with your Streets and Sanitation ward superintendent before putting any large item out**. Start with **311** or your ward/district sanitation office (same path as mattresses and furniture). This applies to DSS-served single-family homes and buildings with four units or fewer. ([Sanitation Code Violation / DSS guidance](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/streets/provdrs/streets_san/svcs/sanitation_ordinance.html))

**What HCCRF will not take:** The Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility **does not accept** household appliances (including refrigerators, washers, dryers, stoves), air conditioners, HVAC equipment, or refrigerants/freons. Do not take freon appliances there. ([HCCRF accepted/not-accepted list](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

**Window AC and other freon units:** The city pages we checked spell out door-removal and bulk-pickup rules for refrigerators and freezers. They do not publish a separate, confirmed process for window air conditioners. **Ask 311** whether your address can put a window AC out on a bulk pickup, and what prep they require.

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

Drop off computers, monitors, TVs, printers, keyboards, game consoles, DVD players, cable boxes and similar devices at the **Household Chemicals & Computer Recycling Facility (HCCRF), 1150 N. North Branch St.**, open **Tuesday 7 a.m.–12 p.m., Thursday 2–7 p.m., and the first Saturday of each month 8 a.m.–3 p.m.** Residential only. Only listed items are accepted, and the City notes that once the truck is full, no more electronics are taken. Don't leave items outside after hours. ([Chicago Recycles: E-Waste](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/chicago-recycles/home/other-wastes.html); [HCCRF](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

The **HCCRF** (same address and hours as above) accepts household chemicals, **oil-based paint, aerosol paint and thinners**, solvents, auto fluids, pesticides, CFLs, many batteries, BBQ propane tanks, and mercury items. It does **not** accept **latex, acrylic or water-based paint**, alkaline batteries, explosives, medical waste, or pharmaceuticals. For latex paint, the City says to use it up, donate it, or **dry it out** (kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust) and put it in the trash. ([HCCRF](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

### How do I schedule a bulk item pickup in Chicago?

Contact 311 (call 311 or use 311.chicago.gov) or your ward's Streets and Sanitation superintendent, say what you're putting out, and wait for instructions before anything goes in the alley or at the curb. City sanitation guidance says to arrange a bulk pick-up before setting out any large item or an extra load of trash, such as a basement or attic clean-out. ([Sanitation Code Violation](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/streets/provdrs/streets_san/svcs/sanitation_ordinance.html))

### Is bulky item pickup free in Chicago?

There's no added charge. The City's garbage fee ($9.50 per dwelling unit per month, or $4.75 with the senior discount) covers all garbage collection at the home, and the City says lawn waste and bulk waste collection cost nothing extra. ([City of Chicago: Garbage Fee FAQ](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/fin/supp_info/garbage-fee-faq.html))

### Does my building qualify for City bulk pickup?

Only if DSS collects your garbage: single-family homes and residential buildings with four units or fewer. Larger apartment buildings and businesses must use a private hauler, so ask your landlord or property manager how bulky items are handled.

### How do I get rid of a refrigerator in Chicago?

Remove the doors or lids first, then arrange a bulk pickup the same way as other large items. Don't take a fridge, freezer, air conditioner or other appliance to the HCCRF: it doesn't accept appliances or refrigerants.

### Where can I drop off a TV or computer in Chicago?

At the HCCRF, 1150 N. North Branch St., for residents only, on Tuesday 7 a.m. to 12 p.m., Thursday 2 to 7 p.m., and the first Saturday of the month 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. Electronics go to the yellow building. Never leave anything outside the gate after hours. ([City of Chicago: HCCRF](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/env/supp_info/environmental-permitting-and-inspection/hccrf.html))

### How do I dispose of paint in Chicago?

Oil-based paint, spray paint and thinners go to the HCCRF's chemical drop-off (the blue building). Latex, acrylic and water-based paint isn't accepted there: use it up, give it away, or mix in kitty litter, shredded paper or sawdust until it's dry, then put it in the trash.

### What won't the City haul away?

Remodeling debris: if you're renovating, removing discarded building materials is your job, and the City suggests renting a dumpster. For that, or if you can't wait for City timing, a local junk removal company listed on this page may help. HowToDumpIt does not haul anything.

## Christmas Trees {#christmas-trees}

**Christmas tree short answer:** Chicago's official program is drop-off only at park recycling corrals. The City's page does not describe curbside Christmas tree pickup. The 2026-27 program runs January 9-23, 2027 (checked November 19, 2026).

**2026-27:** The Department of Streets and Sanitation and the Chicago Park District run Holiday Tree Recycling at 27 locations, 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. Natural trees only, placed inside the marked corral. Remove all tinsel, lights, ornaments, tree stands and plastic bags. Trees are chipped into mulch, which is free at six sites starting Tuesday, January 12, 2027: the Forestry Site at 900 E. 103rd Street; Lincoln Park (east side of Cannon Drive, north of Fullerton Avenue); Margate Park, 4921 N. Marine Drive; Mt. Greenwood Park, 3721 W. 111th Street; North Park Village, 5801 N. Pulaski Road; and Warren Park, 6601 N. Western Avenue. The City says over 23,500 trees were recycled in 2026. ([Chicago Recycles: Holiday Tree Recycling](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/chicago-recycles/home/holiday-tree-recycling.html))

Other drop-off parks include Bessemer Park, 8930 S. Muskegon Avenue; Clark Park, 3400 N. Rockwell Street; Garfield Park, 100 N. Central Park Avenue; Grant Park, 900 S. Columbus Drive; Humboldt Park Boathouse, 1369 N. Sacramento Avenue; Jackson Park, 6300 S. Cornell Avenue; and Portage Park, 4100 N. Long Avenue. The full list is on the City page and can change. For questions, the page lists dsscomposting@cityofchicago.org and 312-744-2413.

For a side-by-side look at every option, including all 27 sites and the Christmas week 2026 schedule, see [Christmas tree recycling in Chicago](/chicago-il/christmas-tree-disposal/).
