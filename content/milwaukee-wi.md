# Junk Disposal in Milwaukee, WI: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, item by item. City of Milwaukee Sanitation Services gives residents two main options. You can set up to **1 cubic yard of bulky items** at your collection point on your **regular garbage day**, or take larger loads to one of the City's **two Drop Off Centers**. **Appliances, TVs and other electronics** are not picked up at the curb and go to a Drop Off Center. **Paint and household chemicals** go to the **Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage District (MMSD)** household hazardous waste sites. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call **414-286-CITY (2489)** before you set anything out.

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Set it at your collection point on your regular garbage day (up to 1 cubic yard free), or take it to a Drop Off Center |
| Couch / furniture | Up to 1 cubic yard goes out free on your regular day; book a paid special pickup or use a Drop Off Center for more |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Not picked up at the curb; take it to a Drop Off Center, and ask the City how to handle the refrigerant first |
| TV / computer | Not picked up at the curb and banned from Wisconsin landfills; Drop Off Centers take up to 3 TVs a visit at $5 each, other electronics free |
| Paint / chemicals | Take oil-based paint and chemicals to an MMSD household hazardous waste site; dry out latex paint and put it in your garbage cart, or take it to MMSD |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** The City treats a **queen mattress** as a bulky item. You can set **1 cubic yard of bulky items** (about the size of a recliner or two garbage carts) at your regular collection point on your regular collection day. You don't need to fill out a request or call the City. ([City of Milwaukee: Bulky Item Pickup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/Bulky-Item-Pickup))

### Step by Step: Bulky Item Pickup

1. **Check that you have City garbage service.** Bulky item pickup is only for properties whose garbage is collected by City of Milwaukee Sanitation Services. The City collects garbage from about 190,000 households in buildings with 1 to 4 units. ([City of Milwaukee: Sanitation Services](https://city.milwaukee.gov/dpwservices/Sanitation))
2. **Keep it to 1 cubic yard for a free pickup.** Put it at your regular collection point on your regular day.
3. **For more than 1 cubic yard, request a special pickup.** Place your items at the collection point, then request a special pickup online or call 414-286-CITY (2489). The City lists **$75 for 1 to 4 cubic yards** and **$225 for 4 to 6 cubic yards**.
4. **Don't set out more than 6 cubic yards or mixed loads.** Piles over 6 cubic yards, loose material, or mixed materials (such as construction waste, recyclables, tires or brush) don't qualify for special pickup, and the City refers the property to the Department of Neighborhood Services as a violation. ([City of Milwaukee: Bulky Item Pickup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/Bulky-Item-Pickup))

Find your collection day with the City's [garbage and recycling schedule lookup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/GarbageRecyclingSchedules).

**Bed bugs or soiled mattresses:** We couldn't find an official City rule about wrapping or bagging a mattress for pickup. Call 414-286-CITY (2489) before you set one out.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Curbside:** Furniture follows the same bulky item rules as mattresses. If it's smaller than a recliner, it goes out with your regular garbage and needs no request. If it's bigger, there's a fee for collection, and the City says a Drop Off Center costs less than booking a pickup. Many local organizations take furniture for reuse, so consider donating first. ([City of Milwaukee: Recycling Directory](https://city.milwaukee.gov/recycle/what/Recycling-Directory))

**Drop Off Centers:** The City runs two centers. They're open to **City of Milwaukee residents and property owners** with current state or City-issued photo ID. If your ID shows an address outside the City, bring a property tax bill or recent utility bill in the same name. Staff don't help unload, and old furniture and mattresses dropped off here are landfilled.

| Drop Off Center | Address | Hours (City Drop Off Centers Page) |
|---|---|---|
| North Side | 6660 N. Industrial Road | Tue to Sun, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. (April 12 to November 28, 2026) |
| South Side | 3879 W. Lincoln Avenue | Tue to Sun, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. (April 12 to November 28, 2026) |

The centers are closed on City holidays, including Thanksgiving (Thursday and Friday) and Christmas Eve and Day. The page lists winter hours of Tuesday to Saturday, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m., for the season that ended April 11, 2026, but it doesn't list dates for this winter yet, so check the page after November 28. Lincoln Avenue construction limits access to the South Side center: you can only reach it heading west on Lincoln from S. 35th Street.

**Drop Off Center fees** (by load size, set by staff):

| Load Size | Fee |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 cubic yard (passenger car) | Free |
| 1 to 3 cubic yards (pickup truck) | $20 |
| 3 to 6 cubic yards (small trailer or tall truck) | $30 |
| 6 to 10 cubic yards (large trailer or small box truck) | $50 |
| 10 to 18 cubic yards (large box truck or enclosed trailer) | $90 |

Normal recyclables and scrap metal are free regardless of volume. The payment booth takes cash, debit and major credit cards. Box trucks or trailers over 16 feet, dump, flatbed, semi and utility trucks, and loads that aren't safely contained aren't allowed. ([City of Milwaukee: Drop Off Centers](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/DropOff))

**Renovation debris:** Construction and demolition waste isn't bulky waste. Take it to a Drop Off Center for a volume-based fee, or book a pre-paid collection, which the City lists at **$50.00 per cubic yard**. A small piece of carpet (the size of an area rug) can go out at the curb. ([City of Milwaukee: Bulky Item Pickup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/Bulky-Item-Pickup)) Shingles, bricks and block, concrete and stones, and dirt must be fully separated at the center. ([City of Milwaukee: Construction Debris](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/constructiondebris))

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Not picked up at the curb:** The City says appliances aren't bulky waste and must go to a Drop Off Center. ([City of Milwaukee: Bulky Item Pickup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/Bulky-Item-Pickup)) That covers air conditioners, washers and dryers, dishwashers, freezers, microwaves, ovens, refrigerators, furnaces, boilers, dehumidifiers, water heaters and stoves. ([City of Milwaukee: Recycling Directory](https://city.milwaukee.gov/recycle/what/Recycling-Directory)) The Drop Off Centers recycle appliances. The City's page doesn't list a separate appliance fee, so ask about the cost when you arrive or call 414-286-CITY (2489) first. ([City of Milwaukee: Drop Off Centers](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/DropOff))

**Freon:** The City's pages don't say whether refrigerant has to be removed before you drop off a fridge, freezer or AC unit, and MMSD's hazardous waste sites don't take refrigerators. ([MMSD: Household Hazardous Waste Collection](https://www.mmsd.com/what-you-can-do/home-haz-mat-collection)) The US EPA says that when refrigerant has to be recovered first, you'd need a professional, such as an appliance repair shop, to remove it. Never cut the lines or let the refrigerant out yourself. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Banned from the trash:** Since September 1, 2010, many electronics have been banned from Wisconsin landfills. City crews won't pick them up, and no one may put them in the trash. The banned list includes **TVs, computers (desktops, laptops and tablets), monitors, keyboards, mice and other accessories, printers, fax machines, DVD players and VCRs, e-readers and cellphones**. ([City of Milwaukee: Electronics](https://city.milwaukee.gov/Recycle/What-Can-I-Recycle/Disposal--Recycling-Guide/Electronics))

**Drop Off Centers:** City residents can take residential electronics to either Drop Off Center. The centers take **up to three TVs per visit at $5 per TV**, and other electronics on the City's list are free. Cellphones aren't accepted at the centers; the City points to Call2Recycle drop-off locations instead. Delete your personal data first.

**Other options:** E-Cycle Wisconsin is the state's manufacturer-funded program for recycling household electronics, and the Wisconsin DNR lists collection sites and mail-back programs. ([Wisconsin DNR: Electronics Recycling](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/Ecycle)) The US EPA also keeps a list of [manufacturer and retailer electronics donation and recycling programs](https://www.epa.gov/recycle/electronics-donation-and-recycling).

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**MMSD household hazardous waste sites:** MMSD takes household chemicals from **households and apartments in Milwaukee County** only. Businesses, contractors, schools, churches, nonprofits and government agencies can't use them. Stay in your vehicle and put your items in the trunk or near the hatchback.

| MMSD Site | Address | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Milwaukee, 13th Street | 6060 S. 13th Street, Milwaukee, WI 53221 | Tue 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.; Sat 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. |
| Milwaukee, Lincoln Avenue | 3879 W. Lincoln Ave., Milwaukee, WI 53215 | Thu, Fri and Sat, 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. |
| Menomonee Falls | W124N9451 Boundary Rd., Menomonee Falls, WI 53051 | Mon and Wed 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.; Sat 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. |

Each site lists its own 2026 closed days, including the Thanksgiving period, so check MMSD's page before you go. Questions: **(414) 225-2066**.

- **Accepted:** latex paint (up to 20 gallons a visit), oil-based paint (no limit), paint thinner and stripper, stains and varnish, antifreeze, gasoline, motor oil and filters, pesticides and weed killer, pool chemicals, cleaners, aerosol cans, rechargeable and button batteries, fluorescent tubes (up to 20 a visit) and CFLs, mercury thermometers, and 1-pound and 20-pound propane tanks.
- **Not accepted:** containers larger than 15 gallons, car batteries, computers, TVs, microwaves, refrigerators, washers and dryers, tires, fire extinguishers, smoke detectors, explosives and ammunition, medical waste and prescription drugs.

([MMSD: Household Hazardous Waste Collection](https://www.mmsd.com/what-you-can-do/home-haz-mat-collection))

**Latex paint at home:** The City says to dry latex paint in the container. Then it can go in your green garbage cart or to a Drop Off Center. Oil-based paint isn't accepted curbside; take it to an MMSD site. Consider donating unopened paint to charity. ([City of Milwaukee: Recycling Directory](https://city.milwaukee.gov/recycle/what/Recycling-Directory))

**Motor oil, antifreeze and car batteries:** The City's Drop Off Centers also recycle these. ([City of Milwaukee: Drop Off Centers](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/DropOff))

Questions? Call **414-286-CITY (2489)**. ([City of Milwaukee: Bulky Item Pickup](https://city.milwaukee.gov/sanitation/Bulky-Item-Pickup))
