# Junk Disposal in Cambridge–Somerville, MA: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Cambridge and Somerville sit side by side, but their junk rules are not the same. This guide covers both cities, item by item, and every option links to the official City of Cambridge or City of Somerville page we checked. Where the two cities differ, we spell it out. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page, call Cambridge Public Works at 617-349-4800, or call Somerville 311 (617-666-3311 from outside Somerville).

One rule covers both cities: Massachusetts bans mattresses, textiles, white goods (large appliances), cathode ray tubes (old tube TVs and monitors) and several other materials from disposal as trash ([MassDEP Waste Disposal Bans](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massdep-waste-disposal-bans)).

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Cambridge | Somerville |
|---|---|---|
| Mattress | Schedule a UTEC curbside pickup; $45 per piece | Buy a $55 Disposal Sticker, then schedule with 311 |
| Couch / furniture | One piece per week on trash day, no permit (some exceptions) | Two pieces per week with regular trash |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Paid appliance permit; fridge/freezer doors removed | $25 White Goods Sticker per item; remove fridge door |
| TV / computer | 20" or smaller at the curb free; bigger TVs/monitors need a paid permit; small electronics at the Recycling Center | Free TV/monitor curbside pickup via 311, or drop off e-waste at DPW Yard, 1 Franey Rd. |
| Paint / chemicals | Dried latex paint in trash, lids off; HHW day Saturday, November 7, 2026 | Dry, empty cans in trash; full cans to HHW drop-off (Thursday, November 5, or Saturday, November 14, 2026) |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Both cities recycle mattresses (state law bans them from the trash), and both now charge for it. **Cambridge:** schedule a curbside pickup with the City's contractor UTEC and pay **$45 per mattress or box spring**. **Somerville:** buy a **$55 Disposal Sticker** for each piece, then schedule the pickup through **311**.

<!--LISTINGS-->

### Cambridge: Step by Step (UTEC)

1. If you live in a building with **more than 12 units**, ask your property manager first. Your building may have its own procedure.
2. Schedule online with UTEC at [utecinc.org/mattress-recycling](https://www.utecinc.org/mattress-recycling/). Book ahead; the City says slots fill up quickly. Since **July 20, 2026**, UTEC charges **$45 per mattress or box spring**, and the City still covers the rest of the cost ([City of Cambridge announcement, July 1, 2026](https://www.cambridgema.gov/departments/publicworks/news/2026/07/changescomingtocambridgemattressandboxspringrecyclingcollectionprogram)). If you're facing financial hardship, there's a **$5 discount**; call UTEC at 978-856-3997.
3. Put the mattress at the curb in front of your address **no earlier than 6 p.m.** the night before and **by 7 a.m.** on pickup day. You don't need to be home. The crew picks up before 3 p.m. and can't come onto private property.
4. Tape a note to it with "UTEC," your order number and the pickup date so the crew grabs the right one.
5. Problems? Email mattress-recycling@utecinc.org and recycle@cambridgema.gov.

UTEC won't take excessively torn, punctured, soiled or moldy mattresses; anything with bed bugs or other pests (now or in the past); futons, mattress pads or toppers; infant sleeping pads; or air or water beds. Email recycle@cambridgema.gov about those. UTEC also doesn't pick up couches, bed frames or appliances. There's no mattress drop-off site in Cambridge itself. Other options the City lists include reselling or giving it away (Facebook Marketplace, Buy Nothing Cambridge, Craigslist) and paid services such as Furnishing Hope, HandUp Mattress Resale & Recycling (Boston-based; on-call service in Somerville only, not Cambridge), Mattress Pro Recycling and Marvin Junk Removal. Contact them first for prices ([Cambridge Mattress Recycling](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/mattressrecycling)).

*Heads-up:* Cambridge's [large-item page](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/furnitureappliancelargeitemdisposal) still describes mattress pickup as free. The City's dedicated mattress page and its July 2026 announcement both list the $45 UTEC fee, so plan on paying it.

### Somerville: Step by Step ($55 Sticker + 311)

1. **Check that you're eligible.** Only homes in buildings with **6 or fewer units** that get regular City trash pickup qualify. Bigger buildings with private trash service should ask the building manager.
2. **Buy a $55 Disposal Sticker for each piece** (one per mattress and one per box spring): online at [somervillema.gov/payonline](https://www.somervillema.gov/payonline), at DPW (One Franey Road; check, credit card or money order, no cash), or at the City Clerk's Office in City Hall (93 Highland Avenue). Stickers ordered online can take **up to 10 days** to arrive, and you can't schedule until the physical sticker is in hand.
3. **Schedule with 311** no later than **3:30 p.m. the day before** collection: call 311 (617-666-3311 from outside Somerville) or use the other options at [somervillema.gov/311](https://www.somervillema.gov/311).
4. **Set it out:** pickup is the **day after your regular trash day** (Friday trash routes get Friday pickup). Put the stickered items on public property at the curb no earlier than **4 p.m.** the day before and **by 7 a.m.** on pickup day, and leave at least a **36-inch** clear path on the sidewalk. Don't stack anything on top.
5. **Limits:** max **2 bulk items per property per week**, and a mattress and a box spring count as two. To cancel, contact 311 by noon the day before.

Not accepted for recycling in Somerville: futons, mattress pads or toppers; infant sleeping pads not meant for cribs; excessively torn, soiled or moldy items; broken, crushed or twisted items; and air or water beds. If a mattress is soiled or may have bed bugs, **call 311 before buying a sticker**, and see [somervillema.gov/bedbugs](https://www.somervillema.gov/bedbugs). Mattresses set out with the trash without a scheduled pickup will be left behind and may be fined ([Somerville Mattress and Box Spring Recycling](https://www.somervillema.gov/mattressrecycling)).

### Need It Gone Sooner?

Both cities point to the same backups: ask the store to take your old mattress when you buy a new one, donate it if it's in excellent shape (Somerville suggests [householdgoods.org](https://www.householdgoods.org)), or hire a private mattress-recycling hauler.

*Sources: [Cambridge Mattress Recycling](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/mattressrecycling), [Somerville Mattress and Box Spring Recycling Program](https://www.somervillema.gov/mattressrecycling). Checked October 13, 2026. Fees and rules change, so confirm before you set anything out.*

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Cambridge:** Furniture such as couches and tables doesn't need a permit. You can put **one piece of furniture out per week on trash day**, though the City notes some exceptions, so check the item in the [Get Rid of It Right tool](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/curbsidecollections) first. Set items out after 6 p.m. the night before or by 7 a.m. on collection day. Some metal items need a free permit for pickup: small exercise equipment, bicycles, lawnmowers, radiators, snow blowers, and metal filing cabinets and desks. Larger motorized exercise equipment needs a **paid** permit ([Cambridge Furniture, Appliance, Large Item Disposal](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/furnitureappliancelargeitemdisposal)).

**Somerville:** The City will pick up **two pieces of furniture per week** with your regular trash. Tape both sides of any mirrors or glass so they don't shatter, and tie down or remove drawers and doors. Rugs and carpets must be rolled, tied, and no more than three feet long. Somerville **won't** take heavy metal items (exercise equipment, exercise bikes, metal cabinets), motorized lawnmowers, snow blowers or construction materials; you have to get rid of those privately. The mattress program's two-bulk-items-per-week limit includes mattresses, so plan ahead if you're clearing out a whole apartment ([Somerville Trash & Recycling](https://www.somervillema.gov/trash-and-recycling)).

Furniture in good shape? Both cities encourage donating first. Somerville lists charities including Goodwill, Salvation Army, Second Chances, Somerville Homeless Coalition and the Society of St. Vincent de Paul. Cambridge suggests posting on NextDoor, Freecycle or Craigslist.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Cambridge:** These need a **disposal permit and a fee**: air conditioners*, clothes washers and dryers, copiers and printers over 40 lbs, dehumidifiers*, refrigerators* and freezers* (non-commercial, **doors removed**), stoves, trash compactors, water coolers and heaters, and larger motorized exercise equipment. Freon is removed from the items marked * to meet the Clean Air Act, and then everything goes to a scrap metal recycler. Apply online with the [Appliance/Large Item Disposal Permit](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/furnitureappliancelargeitemdisposal) or in person at DPW, 147 Hampshire St. (Monday 8:30 a.m.–8 p.m., Tuesday–Thursday 8:30 a.m.–5 p.m., Friday 8:30 a.m.–noon; check, money order, cash or credit card). Most pickups are scheduled for Wednesdays or Thursdays. Set items out by 7 a.m. or after 6 p.m. the night before. No commercial or industrial items. The fee isn't listed on the main City page; you'll see it when you apply, or call DPW at 617-349-4800.

**Somerville:** Large appliances need a **$25 White Goods Sticker** (one per item). Buy it online, at DPW, or at City Hall. Covered items: air conditioners, dryers, refrigerators (**remove the door** before putting it on the sidewalk), stoves, washers, water coolers (bubblers) and water heaters. Don't block the sidewalk when you set it out. The [DPW Sanitation page](https://www.somervillema.gov/departments/dpw/sanitation) links to scheduling a white goods pickup; call 311 if you're not sure how to book ([Somerville Trash & Recycling: Electronic Waste & Appliances](https://www.somervillema.gov/trash-and-recycling)).

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

Old tube (CRT) TVs and monitors are banned from the trash in Massachusetts ([MassDEP](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massdep-waste-disposal-bans)).

**Cambridge:**

- **Small TVs and monitors (20" diagonal or less):** no permit. Put them near the curb edge by 7 a.m. on your regular collection day. You can include the hard drive, keyboard, mouse, and a small printer or fax machine.
- **TVs and monitors over 20":** these need a **paid permit**, the same as appliances ([Cambridge large-item page](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/furnitureappliancelargeitemdisposal)).
- **Small electronics** (microwaves, stereos, box fans, small printers) can go to the curb without a permit, or you can recycle them at the **Recycling Center**, 147 Hampshire St. (back right corner of the DPW yard). It's open Tuesdays and Thursdays 4–7:30 p.m. and Saturdays 9 a.m.–4 p.m. and takes small appliances, computers, cell phones, string lights, batteries (sort alkaline separately), fluorescent bulbs and mercury items. Cambridge residents only. Pop-up Recycle events take the same materials; the next one listed is **Wednesday, October 28, 2026, 4–7:30 p.m. at 25 Corporal McTernan St.** ([Cambridge Recycling Center](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/recyclingcenter)).

**Somerville:**

- **TVs and computer monitors:** call 311 to schedule a **free curbside pickup**, or drop them off at the DPW Yard. Sets with broken glass aren't accepted.
- **E-waste drop-off:** DPW Yard, **1 Franey Rd.**, Monday–Friday **8:30 a.m.–noon and 1–3:30 p.m.** It's for residents only (no contractors), and you'll need to show proof of residency, like a driver's license or utility bill, at the guard shack. Accepted: desktops and laptops (with keyboard and mouse), TVs and monitors, cables, networking gear, printers, copiers and scanners, phones, projectors, battery backups, audio equipment, toner and ink cartridges, DVD/CD/VHS players, and most small plug-in household items such as blenders, microwaves, irons, toasters and coffee makers ([Somerville Electronic Waste Recycling Program](https://www.somervillema.gov/departments/dpw/programs/electronic-waste-recycling-program)).
- The City also notes that retailers like Apple and Best Buy and several phone carriers run their own take-back programs; check with them directly.

Wipe your personal data from phones and computers before you recycle them.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**Cambridge:** Cambridge holds four Household Hazardous Waste (HHW) days a year at **50 Mooney St.**, for Cambridge residents only. Bring proof of residency. The last 2026 date is **Saturday, November 7, 2026, 9 a.m.–1 p.m.** Accepted: vehicle, lithium-ion and alkaline batteries (sorted by type); car fluids; up to four car tires per household; cleaners, glues, removers, and photo and pool chemicals; fluorescent bulbs; mercury thermometers and thermostats; **oil-based and latex paint**; pesticides and weed killers; prescription medicines; propane cylinders (20 lbs or less); waste fuels; and fire extinguishers. *Not* accepted: ammunition, fireworks or explosives; asbestos; bleach or ammonia; commercial waste; construction debris; empty aerosol cans; compressed gas cylinders; infectious, biological or radioactive waste; smoke detectors (those go in the trash); and syringes. Property managers bringing more than 25 pounds or 25 gallons, or anyone without proof of residency, should email recycle@cambridgema.gov ahead of time.

- **Latex paint between events:** dry it out with the lid off (let it evaporate, or add kitty litter or a drying agent), then set the open can next to your trash at the curb.
- **Prescription medicines:** accepted year-round at the Police Department, 125 Sixth St.
- **Can't make the date?** The City lists Clean Harbors in Braintree (1 Hill Ave., 781-380-7100) on Saturday mornings from May to October for a small per-pound fee. Call them first. ([Cambridge Household Hazardous Waste Disposal](https://www.cambridgema.gov/services/householdhazardouswastedisposal))

**Somerville:** HHW drop-off is at the **DPW Yard, 1 Franey Rd.**, on the **second Saturday of each month, April through November, 9 a.m.–noon**, plus weeknight events on Thursday, April 23, and **Thursday, November 5, 2026, 4:30–7:30 p.m.** That leaves **Thursday, November 5** and **Saturday, November 14, 2026** this season, and the [November 14 event listing](https://www.somervillema.gov/events/2026/11/14/hazardous-waste-drop) shows it as free. Bring proof of residency. Bring no more than fits in a car trunk, with everything properly contained and packed in cardboard boxes. Accepted examples include motor oil, aerosol paint, household cleaning chemicals and rechargeable batteries; use the [Somerville Waste Wizard](https://www.somervillema.gov/trash-and-recycling) for the full list.

- **Paint cans:** if they're **dry and empty**, put them in the regular trash. Cans that aren't empty have to go to a Hazardous Waste Day.
- **Batteries:** regular alkaline batteries (A, AA, C, D) can go in the trash. Rechargeable, button-cell and lithium batteries go to Hazardous Waste Days, and laptop battery packs go with e-waste.
- Large or non-empty fire extinguishers go to Hazardous Waste Days. Kerosene, gasoline and heating oil must be disposed of privately ([Somerville Trash & Recycling](https://www.somervillema.gov/trash-and-recycling)).

*Rules, hours, fees and schedules change. Confirm on the linked official page, with Cambridge DPW (617-349-4800), or with Somerville 311 before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
