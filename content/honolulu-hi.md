# Junk Disposal in Honolulu, HI: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in **Honolulu**, item by item. The **City and County of Honolulu** covers the whole island of O'ahu, so these rules apply from Waikiki to Kapolei to the North Shore. Bulky items are picked up **free, by appointment only**: it is illegal to put them at the curb on any other day. You can also drive them to a City **convenience center** or **transfer station**. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Questions? Call the City's Refuse Division at **768-3200**.

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Free bulky item pickup by appointment, or a City convenience center or transfer station |
| Couch / furniture | Free bulky item pickup by appointment (up to 5 items), or drop-off |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Separate metal appliance appointment (up to 2), or a convenience center (not Keehi) |

| Paint / chemicals | Harden or absorb latex and oil paint for the trash; other chemicals by HHW appointment |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Schedule a free **bulky item collection** appointment, or take the mattress to a City convenience center or transfer station, where mattresses go in as regular refuse. ([City and County of Honolulu: How to Dispose Of...](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-how-to-dispose-of-trash/); [Rules and Guidelines for Residents](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-waste-drop-off-rules-residents/))

### Step by Step: Bulky Item Appointment

1. **Book it.** Use the City's [Bulky Item Collector](https://honolulu.sanstar.net/honolulu-public-client/app/appointments/create-appointment) or call **768-3200, option 0**. List every item and how many; only scheduled items are collected.
2. **Know the limits.** One appointment per address per month (Waikiki residents can book weekly). Single-family homes and each apartment unit can set out up to **5 bulky items**, and up to **2 metal appliances** on a separate appliance appointment.
3. **Time it right.** Put items out no earlier than **6 p.m.** the evening before, and by **6 a.m.** on your appointment day.
4. **Placement.** Curbside in front of your property, not blocking sidewalks, driveways, drain inlets or the road. Crews will not enter private property. If your cart is collected from a back alley, put the items there instead.
5. **Size.** Long items and bundles: 6 feet and 60 pounds maximum.

**Don't set out early:** The City says leaving bulky items at the curb outside your appointment date is illegal and can lead to civil fines. If your next appointment is too far away, use a drop-off site. ([Bulky Item Appointment FAQ (PDF)](https://www.honolulu.gov/rep/site/envref/envref_docs/BULKY_APPT_BROCHURE.pdf))

**Bed bugs:** We did not find a City rule for wrapping bed-bug or soiled mattresses. Ask the Refuse Division at 768-3200 before you set one out.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Couches, chairs, tables and other furniture** go out on a **bulky item appointment** (up to 5 items) or to a City drop-off site. ([How to Dispose Of...](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-how-to-dispose-of-trash/)) At drop-off sites, chairs, tables, mattresses and fastened rugs or carpeting go in the regular refuse container. Anything that can be cut or taken apart must be under 6 feet long and under 60 pounds, and you unload it yourself; attendants can't help. ([Rules and Guidelines for Residents](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-waste-drop-off-rules-residents/))

**Drop-off sites (residents):** convenience centers in Waimanalo, Waianae, Waipahu, Ewa Beach, Kapolei, Laie and Wahiawa are open **7 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily**. The transfer stations are **Kapaa** (100 Kapaa Quarry Road, Kailua), **Kawailoa** (62-180 Kawailoa Road, Haleiwa) and **Keehi** (606 Middle Street, Honolulu; closed Sundays). Residents get **two loads per day**, in a sedan, SUV, pickup or minivan; trailers need approval first. All sites are closed on Christmas and New Year's days. ([Waste Drop-off Locations](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/waste-drop-off-locations/))

**Donate first:** The City keeps a list of reuse organizations that take usable furniture and household goods; call before you drop anything off. ([Reuse Organizations](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-reuse-organizations/))

**Apartments and condos:** Each unit can book its own bulky appointment (up to 5 items) and leave items at the building's common pickup spot; ask your resident or property manager where that is. Some buildings (AOAOs) book for the whole building instead. Otherwise, follow your property management's directions.

### Outside City Limits?

There is no "outside city limits" on O'ahu: the **City and County of Honolulu** runs collection and drop-off for the whole island, and every City site is open to O'ahu residents. ([Waste Drop-off Locations](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/waste-drop-off-locations/)) The neighbor islands (Maui, Hawai'i Island and Kaua'i) are separate counties with their own rules. If you live in a condo, apartment, military housing or HOA community with a **private hauler**, ask your property manager or HOA first; the City notes those haulers may follow different holiday schedules. ([2025-2026 Holiday Trash Collection Schedules](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/2025-2026-holiday-trash-collection-schedules/))

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Metal appliances** need their **own appointment**: up to **2 metal appliances** per appliance appointment, separate from your 5-item bulky appointment. ([Bulky Item Collector](https://honolulu.sanstar.net/honolulu-public-client/app/appointments/create-appointment))

**Drop-off:** convenience centers and the Kapaa and Kawailoa transfer stations take household **refrigerators, freezers, air conditioners, washers, dryers, water heaters, stoves and dishwashers**, limit **2 per month**, household types only. **Keehi Transfer Station does not take large metal appliances.** ([Rules and Guidelines for Residents](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-waste-drop-off-rules-residents/); [Waste Drop-off Locations](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/waste-drop-off-locations/))

**Rebate:** The City says qualifying fridges and freezers may get a Hawaii Energy cash rebate when you schedule a free pickup through its Rid-A-Fridge program. Small appliances can go in your regular refuse. ([How to Dispose Of...](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-how-to-dispose-of-trash/))

**Freon / refrigerants:** Never cut the lines or release refrigerant yourself; the US EPA says the final disposer in the disposal chain is responsible for making sure refrigerant is recovered. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Recycle it free at a City site.** Every City convenience center and transfer station has an **e-waste bin** for TVs (9 inches or larger), computers, laptops, monitors, printers, tablets, cords and similar devices; everything collected is recycled. ([E-waste Recycling at City Disposal Sites](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/city-ewaste-dropbins/))

The City also says home electronics may go in regular refuse or a bulky appointment, but asks you to donate or recycle them first; it names Goodwill Hawaii and HawaiianHope for donations (see Other options at the bottom of this page for retail take-back). ([E-Waste Recycling](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-e-waste-recycling/)) Wipe personal data before you let a device go.

**Batteries:** Standalone rechargeable and lithium batteries go to any convenience center or transfer station: tape the terminals and put them in a clear plastic bag. Never put them in the trash. Alkaline batteries can go in regular refuse.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**Latex and oil-based paint:** let it harden in the can, or absorb it, then put it in your regular refuse. **Lead or aluminum paint** goes to a City Household Hazardous Waste (HHW) event (residents only). ([How to Dispose Of...](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-how-to-dispose-of-trash/))

**Household Hazardous Waste events:** pesticides, gasoline, kerosene, solvents, paint thinner and stripper, pool and drain chemicals, CFL bulbs and similar items need an **appointment** at a City HHW drop-off event. Call **(808) 768-3201** or email hhw@honolulu.gov; you'll need your name, address, phone, a list of up to 12 items with container sizes, and a preferred drop-off time between 9 a.m. and 11:45 a.m. The last 2026 event was **Saturday, November 7, 2026** (full), and **2027 event dates were not posted** as of November 19, 2026. ([Household Hazardous Waste](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/opala2-hhw-2/))

**Small amounts:** the City says motor oil, cooking oil and other liquids can be absorbed (oil change box, rags or sawdust in a sealed bag) and put in the trash. Large quantities of any HHW: call the Refuse Division for instructions.

**Propane, helium and fire extinguishers:** convenience centers and the Kapaa and Kawailoa transfer stations take household tanks and extinguishers. Keehi doesn't take compressed gas.

## Christmas Trees {#christmas-trees}

**Christmas tree short answer:** Take off all decorations, cut the tree into sections that fit completely inside your **green cart** with the lid closed, and set it out on your green waste day. Or drop the whole tree at any City convenience center (except Wahiawa), the Kapaa Transfer Station in Kailua, or the City's composting contractor in Wahiawa. ([City and County of Honolulu: Give your Christmas tree a second life](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/give-your-christmas-tree-a-second-life-through-city-recycling-programs/))

**Last season (2025-26, posted December 30, 2025):** Trees with tinsel or flocking had to be cut, bagged and put in the **gray refuse cart**. Homes on manual collection cut trees into **three-foot sections**, bundled them and set them out on their regular refuse day. Live trees without flocking or tinsel could be delivered at no cost to Hawaiian Earth Recycling, 65-1101 Wilikina Drive, Wahiawa. Condos and commercial properties could deliver large loads of trees there with tip fees waived; property managers call (808) 768-3200, option 7. A 2026-27 notice was not posted as of November 19, 2026.

**Holiday collection:** The City's refuse crews observe only two holidays, Christmas Day and New Year's Day. Last season, gray carts due on a holiday stayed out until serviced, while missed blue and green cart pickups were **not** made up, and all City drop-off sites closed on both days. ([2025-2026 Holiday Trash Collection Schedules](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/2025-2026-holiday-trash-collection-schedules/)) The 2026-27 holiday notice was not posted as of November 19, 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

### Does Honolulu pick up bulky items for free?

Yes, by appointment only, booked online or by phone. Single-family homes and each apartment unit can set out up to 5 bulky items per appointment, one appointment per month.

### Can I leave a couch on the curb in Honolulu?

Only on your scheduled bulky item appointment day, from 6 p.m. the night before. Leaving items out at any other time is illegal and can lead to civil fines.

### Where can I drop off junk on Oahu?

At any of the seven City convenience centers (7 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily) or the Kapaa, Kawailoa and Keehi transfer stations. Residents get two loads per day.

### How do I get rid of an old refrigerator in Honolulu?

Book a separate metal appliance appointment (up to 2 appliances), or take it to a convenience center or the Kapaa or Kawailoa transfer station. Keehi doesn't take large appliances.
