# Junk disposal in San Francisco, CA: mattresses, couches, furniture, appliances, TVs & electronics, paint & hazardous waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in San Francisco, California, item by item. The City's garbage company, **Recology**, picks up bulky items at the curb **by appointment at no additional charge**: **two collections per year** for buildings with five units or fewer, and **one per unit** in buildings with six or more units, with **up to 10 items** each time. That covers mattresses, couches, residential appliances (including refrigerators) and electronics. **E-waste is prohibited from your trash and recycling carts**, and paint and chemicals go to the **free Household Hazardous Waste Facility** or Recology's **free home pickup**. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call Recology at (415) 330-1300 before you set anything out.

## Quick answer by item

| Item | Best verified option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | No-charge Recology Bulky Item Collection (up to 10 items, two per year in smaller buildings), retailer take-back with a new mattress, or the SF Transfer Station (first 4 per day free if intact, dry and free of mold and insects) |
| Couch / furniture | No-charge Recology Bulky Item Collection; wrap glass and mirrors and tape display cases; Recology's list excludes pianos, cast iron tubs and anything over 100 pounds |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Residential refrigerators, washers, dryers and stoves go in Recology Bulky Item Collection (no commercial appliances); at the Transfer Station, refrigerators are $73.68 each plus weight |
| TV / computer | Prohibited from trash and recycling carts. Recology Bulky Item Collection, or free drop-off at the SF Transfer Station (TVs of all sizes, monitors, laptops) |
| Paint / chemicals | Free Household Hazardous Waste Facility at 501 Tunnel Ave. (Thursday to Saturday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., 15 gallons or 125 pounds per trip), free Recology home pickup, or up to 5 gallons of paint at a participating hardware store |

## Mattresses and box springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Book a **Recology Bulky Item Collection**. Mattresses and foundations are on the accepted list, and residents get **two no-charge collections per year** (buildings with five units or fewer) or **one per unit** (buildings with six or more units), **up to 10 items** each. If you're buying a new mattress, California law requires the retailer to **offer to take the old one back** at no additional cost.

### Step by step: Recology Bulky Item Collection

1. Check that you qualify. The program is **only for residents of the City of San Francisco**, and the property must have an **active residential or apartment account** with Recology Sunset Scavenger or Recology Golden Gate. Tenants in an apartment building get **one collection per year** and can contact Recology directly to schedule. Unused collections don't carry over. ([Recology FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/))
2. Schedule with Recology at **(415) 330-1300** or **customerservice@recologySF.com**, or with the online Bulky Item Recycling form. Be ready to list all of your items. Recology recommends calling **at least two weeks** before you're ready. ([Recology: Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/bulky-items/), [How do I schedule a Bulky Item Recycling pickup?](https://www.recology.com/faq/sf-bulky-item-collection/), [SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))
3. You can leave **up to 10 items** per appointment. You can use both of your yearly collections at once (up to 20 items), depending on availability. ([Recology: Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/bulky-items/), [Recology FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/))
4. Set items out neatly at the curb in front of your residence **no earlier than 6 p.m. the day before** and **no later than 6 a.m.** on collection day. Don't block the sidewalk. ([Recology FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/), [Recology: Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/bulky-items/))
5. Write **RECOLOGY** on a piece of paper and attach it to one of your items, so City agencies know the pile is scheduled for pickup. ([Recology FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/))
6. Need to cancel? Call or email Recology **by noon the business day before** your pickup, or use the cancel option in your online confirmation email. ([Recology: Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/bulky-items/))

**Need it gone sooner, or used up your collections?** Recology says it can't schedule more than your yearly allotment and won't take payment to collect sooner. Its suggested options are the **SF Transfer Station** (fees may apply; see the listing above), **local charities**, or a **junk removal company**. ([Recology FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/))

### What not to do

- **Don't** put a mattress on the sidewalk without a scheduled pickup. You must schedule before placing items at the curb. ([SF Environment: bulky items](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))
- **Don't** dump it. To report a dumped mattress or furniture on a street or sidewalk, use SF311 (dial 311, or 415-701-2311 from outside San Francisco); the City's expected response time for a mattress is 4 business hours. You can also report the person or business who dumped it. ([Request street or sidewalk cleaning](https://www.sf.gov/request-street-or-sidewalk-cleaning), [Report illegal dumping activity](https://www.sf.gov/report-illegal-dumping-activity))

At the Transfer Station, mattresses are taken at no charge only if they're **intact, reasonably dry, free of mold, insects, and severe damage and stains**. We couldn't find an official San Francisco rule for bed-bug mattresses in curbside collection, so ask Recology before you book.

## Couches and furniture {#furniture}

**Curbside:** Living room furniture, display cases, garage items and other household furniture are on Recology's [list of accepted bulky items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/accepted-bulky-items/). Book them the same way as a mattress (up to 10 items per appointment). Prep rules from Recology's [FAQ](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/residential-curbside-collection/):

- **Glass, ceramic and mirrored items** (for example a china cabinet or mirror): wrap them to prevent injuries and spills.
- **Living room furniture or display cases:** wrap with cardboard or tape together to prevent breakage.
- **Clean textiles** are accepted if bagged or boxed for reuse.

**Not accepted curbside:** pianos, tree trunks, tires and car parts, cast iron tubs, **anything over 100 pounds**, concrete, plaster, dirt and construction debris, and hazardous waste. Extra household garbage and recyclables go in your carts or can be scheduled by emailing customerservice@recologysf.com. ([Accepted Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/accepted-bulky-items/))

**Donate or reuse first:** Recology and SF Environment both encourage donating usable furniture to a San Francisco nonprofit. SF Environment's [SFRecycles](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items) directory lists drop-off and pickup locations that take specific items. Call ahead to confirm what they accept. ([Recology: Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/bulky-items/), [SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))

**Drop-off:** The **SF Transfer Station**, 501 Tunnel Ave., is open to all City residents and businesses seven days a week. General refuse is **$274.57 per ton**, with a **$60 minimum disposal fee per load**. Loads must be tarped and tied down, or Recology adds a $20 fee. ([SF Transfer Station](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/sf-transfer-station/), [SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))

## Appliances (fridges, freezers, AC: freon) {#appliances}

**Curbside:** Recology's Bulky Item Collection accepts **residential refrigerators, residential washers and dryers, residential stoves, kitchen appliances and other appliances**. **Commercial refrigerators, washers, stoves and vending machines are not accepted.** ([Accepted Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/accepted-bulky-items/))

- Recology's pages don't publish prep rules for refrigerators (such as removing food or taping doors), and they don't say whether window or portable AC units count as accepted "appliances." Ask Recology at (415) 330-1300 when you book.

**Drop-off at the SF Transfer Station** (fees from Recology's posted schedule): ([SF Transfer Station](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/sf-transfer-station/))

- **Refrigerators and freon removal:** **$73.68 each** as a surcharge, plus weight.
- **Washers, dryers, stoves, dishwashers:** **$31.22 each** as a surcharge, plus weight.
- A **$60 minimum disposal fee** applies to all loads.

## TVs, computers and electronics (e-waste) {#electronics}

**Electronics can't go in your carts.** Recology says electronic and universal waste, such as consumer batteries, cell phones and TVs, is **prohibited from the trash and recycling carts** because it contains heavy metals such as lead, mercury and cadmium. ([SF Transfer Station](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/sf-transfer-station/))

- **Recology Bulky Item Collection:** electronics are on the accepted list. ([Accepted Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/accepted-bulky-items/))
- **SF Transfer Station, no charge:** **TVs of all sizes** (CRT, plasma and LCD), **computer monitors** of all sizes, **laptops and other LCD devices**, and **small electronic devices and corded appliances**. Bring them as a **separate load**: Recology says non-commercial, California-origin TVs and displays mixed with other trash are charged at the regular disposal rate. Commercial equipment and devices are $74.93 each. ([SF Transfer Station](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/sf-transfer-station/))
- **Batteries:** put household batteries in a tightly sealed plastic bag on top of your closed landfill bin (tape lithium battery terminals), or drop them at any San Francisco Walgreens, most hardware stores or the Household Hazardous Waste Facility. ([Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/))

If a device still works, SF Environment suggests donating it. Wipe personal data from phones and computers before you let them go. ([SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))

## Paint and household hazardous waste {#hazardous}

Hazardous items can't go in your black, green or blue bin; SF Environment says doing so is **illegal**. San Francisco residents have three free options. ([SF Environment: household hazardous waste](https://www.sfenvironment.org/hhw-safe-disposal))

**1. Household Hazardous Waste Facility (drop-off):** 501 Tunnel Ave., San Francisco, CA 94134, next to the public dump. ([SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/hhw-safe-disposal), [Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/))

- **Hours:** Thursday, Friday and Saturday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Closed New Year's Day, Fourth of July, Thanksgiving, the Friday after Thanksgiving, December 25 and December 26.
- **San Francisco residents only.** Bring **proof of residency** (driver's license or utility bill).
- **Limit:** **15 gallons or 125 pounds** per trip. No drums, compressed gas cylinders (except small or BBQ-size propane) or containers larger than 5 gallons. Keep items in their original containers and never mix products.
- **Accepted** (SF Environment's list): household batteries, chemical sprays, liquids and powders, house paint, spray paint, motor oil, antifreeze, solvents, cooking oil, grease and fats, light bulbs (CFLs, tubes and LEDs), BBQ 20-pound propane tanks, empty helium tanks, syringes and needles, unusable art supplies and e-bike batteries. ([Recology SF HHW Facility on SFRecycles](https://www.sfenvironment.org/sfrecycles/vendor/recology-sf-household-hazardous-waste-facility))

**2. Free home pickup:** email **hhw@recology.com** with your name, address, phone number and a photo of your items, or call **415-330-1405** and leave a message. Appointments are Wednesday to Saturday mornings, and **you must be home** (containers can't be left on the sidewalk). Priority goes to disabled and elderly residents who don't drive. Home pickup can't take medications, needles, or unknown or unlabeled chemicals. ([SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/hhw-safe-disposal), [Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/), [SF Environment HHW fact sheet](https://www.sfenvironment.org/files/fliers/files/sfe_hhw_2022_fact_sheet_english_0.pdf))

**3. Paint at a hardware store:** Recology lists participating stores that take **up to 5 gallons of paint per resident**, including Center Hardware (999 Mariposa St.), Cole Hardware (3312 Mission St., 2254 Polk St., 956 Cole St. and 70 4th St.), Cliff's Variety (479 Castro St.), Fredericksen Hardware (3029 Fillmore St.), Golden City Building Supply (1279 Pacific Ave.), Roberts Hardware (1629 Haight St.) and Last's Paint Clearance Center (2141 Mission St.). Call the store before you go. ([Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/))

**Leftover latex paint:** Recology lists paint as hazardous waste that isn't accepted in bulky collection, and we didn't find an official San Francisco rule allowing dried paint in the black bin. Use one of the free options above. The HHW Facility also gives away **free recycled paint** on a first-come, first-served basis. ([Recology: Accepted Bulky Items](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/accepted-bulky-items/), [Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/))

**Medications** aren't accepted at the HHW Facility. Take them to any San Francisco police station or a participating pharmacy (see sfenvironment.org/medicinedisposal). ([Recology: Hazardous Waste](https://www.recology.com/recology-san-francisco/hazardous-waste/))

Not sure where something goes? Call Recology customer service at (415) 330-1300, search SF Environment's SFRecycles directory, or contact SF Environment at (415) 355-3700. ([SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/bulky-items))

*Rules, fees and schedules change. Confirm on the linked official page or with Recology at (415) 330-1300 before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
