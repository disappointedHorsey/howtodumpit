# Junk Disposal in Baltimore, MD: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Baltimore, Maryland, item by item. The Baltimore City Department of Public Works (DPW) gives residents two main options. You can **book bulk trash collection through 311** (up to 3 items, picked up on one Saturday each month), or **drop items off free at a Residential Drop-Off Center** with a valid ID. **Paint and household chemicals** go to the City's **household hazardous waste days** at Sisson Street, not the curb. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call **311** (from outside the city, **(443) 263-2220**) before you set anything out.

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Book bulk trash collection through 311, or recycle a clean, dry mattress free at Quarantine Road (6100 Quarantine Road) |
| Couch / furniture | Book bulk trash collection through 311 (up to 3 items a month) or take it to a free Residential Drop-Off Center |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Book bulk trash collection through 311 or drop it at a Residential Drop-Off Center; the City's pages don't cover refrigerant removal, so ask 311 first |
| TV / computer | Take it to a free Residential Drop-Off Center for eCycling, or use a free manufacturer program listed by the state |
| Paint / chemicals | Take oil paint and chemicals to Sisson Street on a household hazardous waste day (November 6 and 7 or December 4 and 5, 2026); never put them out with bulk trash |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** The City lists **mattresses and box springs** as bulk items it picks up when you book bulk trash collection through 311. ([Baltimore City DPW: Bulk Trash Pickup and Large Item Removal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/bulk-trash-pickup-and-large-item-removal)) If your mattress is clean and dry, you can recycle it instead at the Quarantine Road center.

### Step by Step: Bulk Trash Collection

1. **Find your bulk day.** Bulk trash is collected on **one set Saturday each month**. Call **311** or use [311 Online](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/311services) to find yours.
2. **Book at least 4 working days ahead.** Give 311 your address, your phone number and the items you want picked up. You can put out **up to 3 bulk items a month**.
3. **Label it.** Mark every item **"bulk collection"** and place it where your regular trash is picked up. If you have rear pickup, leave items at the end of the yard and leave the gate unlocked.
4. **Set it out by 7 a.m.** on your pickup day and keep pets indoors.

([Baltimore City DPW: Bulk Trash Pickup and Large Item Removal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/bulk-trash-pickup-and-large-item-removal))

**Recycling a mattress at Quarantine Road:** Baltimore City residents can drop off **clean and dry** mattresses and box springs at **6100 Quarantine Road**, where the City's partner Turbo Haul recycles them. On-site staff will point you to the drop-off area, and you unload the mattress yourself. Sheets, pillowcases, blankets and other bedding can't go in the mattress unit. ([Baltimore City DPW: Mattress and Box Spring Recycling](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/mattress-and-box-spring-recycling)) The mattress page lists hours of **Monday to Saturday, 7:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.**, while the City's drop-off locations page lists **9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.** for Quarantine Road, so plan to arrive after 9 a.m. or call **(410) 396-3772**. ([Baltimore City DPW: Residential Recycling Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/recycle-drop-off-centers))

**Bed bugs or soiled mattresses:** We couldn't find an official City rule about wrapping or bagging mattresses for bulk trash. Ask 311 before you set one out. Quarantine Road takes only clean, dry mattresses for recycling.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Bulk trash collection:** **Sofas, chairs and other furniture** are bulk items, with the same 3-items-a-month limit, 311 booking and set-out rules as mattresses above. The City won't pick up building materials (sheetrock, siding, wood pieces, roofing), auto parts, paint or stain, or flammable or hazardous waste. ([Baltimore City DPW: Bulk Trash Pickup and Large Item Removal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/bulk-trash-pickup-and-large-item-removal))

**Free Residential Drop-Off Centers:** Baltimore City residents with a **valid ID** can drop off **furniture and other bulk items**, household trash, appliances, scrap metal, tires without rims, electronics, recyclables, up to 5 quarts of used motor oil and bagged yard waste **at no cost**. ([Baltimore City DPW: How to Use Drop-Off Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/drop-off-center-guide))

| Drop-Off Center | Address | Phone | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarantine Road Landfill & Residential Recycling Center | 6100 Quarantine Road | (410) 396-3772 | Mon to Sat, 9 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. |
| Western Residential Recycling Center (Reedbird, limited schedule during modernization) | 701 Reedbird Ave. | (410) 396-3367 | Sat, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. |
| Eastern Residential Recycling Center | 6101 Bowley's Lane | (410) 396-9950 | Mon to Sat, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. |
| Sisson Street Residential Recycling Center (no tires) | 2840 Sisson St. | (410) 396-7250 | Mon to Sat, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. |
| Northwest Transfer Station | 5030 Reisterstown Road | (410) 396-2706 | Mon to Sat, 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. |

([Baltimore City DPW: Residential Recycling Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/recycle-drop-off-centers))

**Drop-off rules:** Residents can bring **no more than 2 loads a day and 5 loads a week**, in a passenger car, unmodified van or minivan, or an unmodified pickup no larger than 3/4 ton. Stay with your vehicle, and put each item in its marked container. Commercial vehicles, modified pickups and trailers need a small hauler permit. ([Baltimore City DPW: How to Use Drop-Off Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/drop-off-center-guide))

**Renovation debris:** Construction and demolition materials aren't accepted with bulk trash. Residents can take them only to the **Quarantine Road Landfill scale house**, for **$135 per ton**. ([Baltimore City DPW: How to Use Drop-Off Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/drop-off-center-guide))

**Hiring help:** The City notes that residents can also hire a small hauler for bulk items, and asks you to check where they take your items to help prevent illegal dumping. ([Baltimore City DPW: Bulk Trash Pickup and Large Item Removal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/bulk-trash-pickup-and-large-item-removal))

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Two City options:** Refrigerators, washers, dryers, stoves, ovens, dishwashers, **air conditioners**, grills and water heaters are "white goods" that the City recycles. Baltimore City residents can **drop them off at a Residential Recycling Center** or **call 311 to schedule bulk collection**, placing them where regular trash and recycling are collected. Not sure an item qualifies? Email **recycling@baltimorecity.gov**. ([Baltimore City DPW: White Goods and Scrap Metal Recycling](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/recycling-services/white-goods-scrap-metal-recycling))

**Freon:** The City's pages don't say whether you need to have the refrigerant removed before pickup or drop-off. The US EPA says that when a town requires refrigerant to be recovered first, you'd need a professional, such as an appliance repair shop, to remove it. Never cut the lines or let the refrigerant out yourself. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal)) Ask 311 how to prepare a fridge, freezer or AC unit before you set it out.

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Take them to a Residential Drop-Off Center.** DPW's eCycling program takes **TVs, computer monitors, computers, laptops and tablets, printers, keyboards, phones, cable boxes, game consoles, DVD players, small kitchen appliances such as countertop microwaves, and more**. ([Baltimore City DPW: Electronics Recycling](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/electronics-recycling)) The drop-off guide lists electronics among the items residents can bring free. ([Baltimore City DPW: How to Use Drop-Off Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/drop-off-center-guide)) Delete any personal data from computers and phones first.

**Free manufacturer programs:** The Maryland Department of the Environment keeps a list of [electronics manufacturers that will recycle your old device for free](https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/land/RecyclingandOperationsprogram/Pages/registeredmanu.aspx). ([Maryland MDE: Electronics Recycling (eCycling) in Maryland](https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/land/RecyclingandOperationsprogram/Pages/eCycling.aspx))

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**Not with bulk trash or at regular drop-off.** Bulk trash collection doesn't take paint or stain, and the Residential Drop-Off Centers don't take paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, gasoline, batteries, propane or helium tanks, fire extinguishers or fluorescent bulbs on regular days. ([Baltimore City DPW: How to Use Drop-Off Centers](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/drop-off-center-guide))

**Household hazardous waste days at Sisson Street:** Baltimore City residents can drop off household hazardous waste at the **Sisson Street Residential Recycling Center, 2840 Sisson St.**, phone **(410) 396-7250**. Collections run on the **first Friday and Saturday of each month, June through December**. The City's remaining 2026 dates are:

- **November 6 and 7, 2026**, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
- **December 4 and 5, 2026**, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Businesses must use a private vendor, and commercial vehicles aren't allowed. Everything must be in its **original container with the manufacturer's label**. Unmarked or leaking containers are turned away. ([Baltimore City DPW: Household Hazardous Waste Disposal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/recycling-services/household-hazardous-waste-recycling))

**Accepted:** automotive fluids (antifreeze, transmission and brake fluid, used motor oil), batteries, household cleaning products, fungicides, herbicides, insecticides and pesticides, **oil paint**, paint thinners, pool chemicals and cleaners, propane tanks, rust removers, solvents, fire extinguishers, thermometers, and turpentine, varnishes and stains.

**Not accepted:** photographic chemicals, gasoline, kerosene, asbestos, ammunition, explosives, acids, industrial and medical waste, and radioactive materials. For these, the City points to the Maryland Recycles directory of private companies, some of which charge a fee. ([Baltimore City DPW: Household Hazardous Waste Disposal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/recycling-services/household-hazardous-waste-recycling))

**Latex paint:** The City's hazardous waste list names oil paint but not latex paint, and we couldn't find an official City rule on drying latex paint out for the trash. Ask 311 or call Sisson Street before you go.

Questions? Call **311** (from outside the city, **(443) 263-2220**) or use [311 Online](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/311services). ([Baltimore City DPW: Bulk Trash Pickup and Large Item Removal](https://www.baltimorecity.gov/publicworks/trash-recycling/bulk-trash-pickup-and-large-item-removal))
