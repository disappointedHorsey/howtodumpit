# Junk Disposal in Boston, MA: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Boston, Massachusetts, item by item. Boston Public Works **won't take mattresses with your regular trash**. You **book a curbside mattress pickup** instead, because Massachusetts bans mattresses from disposal. **Furniture** can go out **with your normal curbside trash** on your collection day. **Fridges, freezers, air conditioners, TVs and computer monitors** need a **scheduled special item pickup** through the City or 311. **Oil-based paint and household chemicals** go to the City's **Zero Waste Day** drop-offs, and dried-out latex paint can go in the trash. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call Boston 311 (dial 311 or 617-635-4500) before you set anything out.

Live in **Cambridge or Somerville**? Those cities run their own programs. See our [Cambridge–Somerville guide](/cambridge-somerville-ma/).

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** The City of Boston **requires an appointment** for all mattresses and box springs. It **won't collect them with your curbside trash**. Schedule a curbside pickup online or through 311. The City only picks up mattresses from residents in **buildings with 6 units or fewer**. ([City of Boston: Mattress Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling))

### Step by Step: City Curbside Mattress Pickup

1. **Check that you qualify.** Your building must have **6 units or fewer**. In a bigger building, ask your property manager how to recycle your mattress. ([City of Boston: Mattress Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling))
2. **Book an appointment.** Use the scheduler on the City's mattress page, or call **311** or **617-635-4500** (email 311@boston.gov). If the scheduler can't find your address, the page links to a form. The City **won't collect any mattress without an appointment**.
3. **Bag it if it's soiled.** Mattresses that are **soiled, dirty or have bed bugs must be bagged and fully sealed**, or the pickup may be missed. Mattress bags are sold at hardware stores and online. In bad weather, the City suggests bagging any mattress so it can be fully recycled.
4. **Set it out on time.** Put it at the curb **by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before**, where your trash and recycling usually go. Items **on private property won't be collected**. Don't block it with cars or bins, and don't put anything on top of it.
5. **Don't cut or crush it.** Leave the mattress or box spring whole.

([City of Boston: Mattress Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling))

**What the City takes:** memory foam, box springs, innerspring, hybrid and latex mattresses. **Not** futons, mattress toppers or similar non-mattress items. The City says those can go out with your curbside trash.

**Limits and changes:** Curbside mattress pickup is limited to **10 items per calendar year**. To cancel, use the link in your confirmation email **before 12 p.m. the day before** your appointment, or call 311. If your mattress isn't picked up, **call 311 within 24 hours** of your scheduled date, or you'll have to rebook. ([City of Boston: Mattress Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling))

**What it costs:** The City's mattress page doesn't list a fee. Check the page or call 311 if you're unsure.

**Buying a new mattress?** The City suggests asking the store whether it will take your old mattress for recycling when it delivers the new one.

**Why it's an appointment:** Massachusetts banned mattresses from disposal (trash) as of **November 1, 2022**. ([MassDEP Waste Disposal Bans](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massdep-waste-disposal-bans)) The City says roughly 75 percent of mattress and box spring parts can be taken apart and recycled. ([City of Boston: Mattress Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/mattress-recycling))

**Buildings with 7 or more units:** The City doesn't collect mattresses from these buildings. Boston Public Works posts a list of local mattress recyclers and haulers, some with prices, on its large-building page. ([City of Boston: Trash and Recycling in Large Residential Buildings](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/large-residential-buildings))

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Curbside:** The City says **furniture can be put out with your normal curbside trash and recycling on your scheduled collection day**. ([City of Boston: Trash](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/trash)) No appointment is needed for furniture. The City lists it as "donate or curbside trash." ([City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items))

**Set-out rules for trash day:**

- Put items out **no later than 6 a.m.** on your collection day, and **not before 5 p.m.** the night before. Early set-out can bring a **$25 fine**.
- Find your collection day with the City's [Waste Collection Schedule and Search](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/trash-and-recycling-day-schedule-and-search) or the free Trash Day app.

([City of Boston: Trash](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/trash), [City of Boston: Trash and Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/trash-and-recycling))

**What the City won't take:** **construction debris, automotive parts, plumbing fixtures, fences, water heaters or fire debris**. Hire a private hauler for these. The City also lists **large exercise equipment** and **dishwashers** as "donate or hire a private hauler." ([City of Boston: Trash](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/trash), [City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items))

**Illegal dumping** (vacant lots, public property, parks and playgrounds) can bring a fine of **$100 to $10,000**. ([City of Boston: Trash and Recycling](https://www.boston.gov/trash-and-recycling))

**Still usable?** The City asks you to think about reuse or donation before you trash something. Its [Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/bostons-zero-waste-directory) lists places to donate or recycle items that are too good to trash.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Massachusetts law bans throwing items with freon in the trash.** The City of Boston offers a **special item collection** for them, **by appointment only**. ([City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items)) Large appliances ("white goods") are also on the state's list of materials banned from disposal. ([MassDEP Waste Disposal Bans](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massdep-waste-disposal-bans))

**Items that need a special pickup:** fridges and freezers, water coolers, ice makers, air conditioners and dehumidifiers.

**How to get it picked up:**

1. **Book it** with the scheduler on the City's Special Collection Items page, or call **311** or **617-635-4500**.
2. **Remove all fridge and freezer doors** (mini fridges too). Put the doors next to your trash bin for regular trash pickup. **Remove all food.** The City **won't collect** a fridge or freezer with the doors on or food inside.
3. **Set it out** where your trash usually goes (curb, public alley or dumpster area) **by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before**. It must be in a publicly accessible spot, not on private property, and not blocked or covered.
4. **Don't smash it.** Items must be intact.

**Limits:** **10 special items per calendar year** and **up to 5 per appointment**. No commercial-grade items. Cancel **before 12 p.m. the day before** your appointment. Report a missed pickup to 311 **within 24 hours**. ([City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items))

**What it costs:** The City's special item page doesn't list a fee. Check the page or call 311.

**Water heaters, plumbing fixtures and dishwashers** aren't part of special collection. The City says to donate them or hire a private hauler. ([City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items)) We couldn't find an official City rule for washers and dryers. Check the City's Trash Day app directory or ask 311.

**Big building?** If your building of 7 or more units gets City trash service, call 311 to book special items. ([City of Boston: Trash and Recycling in Large Residential Buildings](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/large-residential-buildings))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Curbside by appointment:** **TVs, laptops and computer monitors** are on the City's special item list, because state law bans throwing cathode ray tubes (CRTs, found in older TVs and monitors) in the trash. Book through the City's Special Collection Items page or 311, with the same rules and limits as appliances (10 items a year, up to 5 per appointment). **TVs and computers must be freestanding, not in a box**, and you should **wipe any data from your computer** first. ([City of Boston: Special Collection Items](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/special-collection-items)) CRTs are on MassDEP's list of materials banned from disposal. ([MassDEP Waste Disposal Bans](https://www.mass.gov/guides/massdep-waste-disposal-bans))

**Zero Waste Day drop-off:** The City's Zero Waste Days take electronics such as **cell phones, computers, computer monitors and TVs, cords and chargers, keyboards, microwaves, printers, tablets and VCR/DVD players**. ([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Offs](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day)) See the paint section below for the date, location and rules.

**Other options:** Many retailers accept some electronics for recycling. The City's [Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/bostons-zero-waste-directory) lists locations. Programs change, so call before you go.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

The City says **latex and acrylic (water-based) paint is not hazardous**. **Let it dry out in the can and put it in your regular trash.** Only **oil-based paint** has to go to a drop-off event. ([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Offs](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day))

**Zero Waste Days** are the City's drop-off events for household hazardous materials and certain banned items. They take **oil-based paint, motor oil, pesticides, aerosol cans, flammable liquids, pool chemicals, rechargeable and lithium batteries, car batteries, propane tanks, fire extinguishers, tires (up to 8 per person), fluorescent bulbs and other intact mercury items**, plus electronics, paper shredding and textiles.

**Next date listed (checked October 24, 2026):** **Saturday, November 21, 2026, 8:30 a.m. to 12 p.m.**, at **Millennium Park, 315 Gardner Street, West Roxbury, MA 02132**. This event was moved from September 26 because of a nor'easter. The City lists it as **free**. ([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Off (Rescheduled)](https://www.boston.gov/calendar/zero-waste-day-drop-rescheduled)) Check the City's Zero Waste Day page for pop-up events and the next year's schedule.

**Rules for Zero Waste Days:**

- **Boston residents only.** Bring a **photo ID, utility bill or lease** as proof. **No business waste.**
- **20-gallon limit** on paint, motor oil, gasoline and all chemicals, with **no container over 5 gallons**. Paints and liquids must have **labels the City can read**.
- **Don't arrive before 8:30 a.m.** Drop-off stops **promptly at 12 p.m.**
- **Not accepted:** asbestos, explosives, radioactive material, commercial or industrial waste, construction debris, alkaline batteries and medical waste.

([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Offs](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day))

**Alkaline batteries** (regular AAA, AA, C, D and 9-volt) **aren't hazardous**. The City says to put them in your regular trash. ([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Offs](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day))

**Can't make it to an event?** The City points you to its [Zero Waste Directory](https://www.boston.gov/bostons-zero-waste-directory) for other places to drop off electronics, batteries, light bulbs and more.

Questions? Contact Boston 311 (dial **311** or **617-635-4500**, email 311@boston.gov) or the Zero Waste team at zerowaste@boston.gov. ([City of Boston: Zero Waste Day Drop-Offs](https://www.boston.gov/departments/public-works/zero-waste-day))

*Rules, schedules and event dates change. Confirm on the linked official page or with Boston 311 before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
