# Junk Disposal in Minneapolis, MN: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in the **City of Minneapolis**, Minnesota, item by item. If your home has **City garbage service**, crews take up to **two large items each week**, and you get up to **six cleanup vouchers a year** for the City's South Transfer Station. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Questions? Call Solid Waste & Recycling at **612-673-2917** (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.).

**City service only.** City pickup serves 1–4 unit residential buildings and some larger properties. If your building uses dumpsters or carts from a private hauler, you don't have City service; see Outside City Limits below. ([City of Minneapolis: Solid Waste & Recycling](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/government/departments/public-works/solid-waste-recycling/))

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | City pickup on your recycling week at no extra charge (kept dry), or the South Transfer Station with a voucher |
| Couch / furniture | City pickup on your garbage day at no extra charge (hide-a-beds and recliners on recycling week only) |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | City pickup on your recycling week; remove fridge and freezer doors |
| TV / computer | City pickup on your recycling week; never in your carts |
| Paint / chemicals | Free Hennepin County drop-off in Bloomington or Brooklyn Park |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Set mattresses and box springs out by **6 a.m. on your recycling week**, with a note that says **"For Solid Waste."** Each one counts toward your two large items for the week. The City collects them for recycling. ([City of Minneapolis: Large Item Collection](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/large-items/))

### Step by Step: Large Item Pickup

1. **Find your week.** Look up your garbage and recycling days with the City's [pickup day lookup](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/collection-day-lookup/). Mattresses, appliances, electronics, metal items, hide-a-beds and recliners go out on your **recycling week** only.
2. **Label it.** Attach a note to each item that says "For Solid Waste." Unmarked items may not be collected.
3. **Keep the mattress dry.** Protect it from rain and snow. Waterlogged or frozen mattresses are too heavy and can't be collected for recycling.
4. **Set it out by 6 a.m.** at your curb or alley collection point, no more than two large items a week. If you want to set a mattress out at 6 a.m. on the day it will actually be collected, call the office on or before your recycling day.
5. **Leave it until it's gone.** Recyclable items get a "We'll be back" sticker and are picked up the next business day. The City notes that large recyclable item collection is currently delayed in some parts of the city, so leave items at your collection point until they're picked up.
6. **Missed pickup?** Call the office within two business days. If the item wasn't out when the crew came by, they won't come back.

**Bed bugs:** We did not find a Minneapolis rule for wrapping bed-bug or soiled mattresses. Ask Solid Waste & Recycling at 612-673-2917 before you set one out.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Garbage day:** Wood or upholstered furniture, wood tables, plastic items and couches go out on your **garbage day**, by 6 a.m., labeled "For Solid Waste," up to two large items a week. Couches must be **under six feet long**. **Hide-a-beds and recliners** go out on your recycling week only. Don't take furniture apart: crews may tag broken-down pieces as building materials, which the City does not pick up. Rolled carpet must be under five feet long, under 12 inches across and under 40 pounds, tied with rope, twine or tape (no wire). ([City of Minneapolis: Large Item Collection](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/large-items/))

**More than two items?** Haul them yourself to the **South Transfer Station**, 2850 20th Ave S (City customers only):

- **Hours:** Tuesday to Friday 12:30 to 7:30 p.m., Saturday 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Items are accepted until 7:15 p.m. (3:15 p.m. on Saturday).
- **Voucher first:** the utility bill payer must call 612-673-2917 during office hours to request a voucher before you go. Each of your **six cleanup vouchers** a year covers one trip with up to 2,000 pounds of garbage and building materials plus up to two large recyclable items.
- **At the station:** show a current state-issued ID, weigh in and weigh out, and unload yourself; staff can't help. No commercial vehicles; the limit is 25 feet long by 11 feet high, including any trailer.
- **Over the limit:** a $15 administrative fee plus $25 per extra appliance, electronic item, mattress or box spring, or $41 per ton over 2,000 pounds, all added to your utility bill. Cash, checks and credit cards aren't accepted.
- **No City garbage service but a Minneapolis water bill?** Ask for a pay per use pass: $15 administrative fee, $40 minimum, and $25 per appliance, electronic item, mattress or box spring.

([City of Minneapolis: Drop-Off Items and Fees](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/garbage/garbage-drop-off-site/drop-off-items-and-fees/); [Vouchers for Garbage Drop-Off](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/garbage/garbage-drop-off-site/garbage-vouchers/); [How to Go to the Drop-Off Site](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/garbage/garbage-drop-off-site/garbage-dropoff-guidelines/))

**Moving out or a big cleanout?** The City offers a paid **special cleanup**: apply at least 5 days ahead, have everything out by 6 a.m. the business day before, and expect a $100 minimum billed at $181 per hour on your utility bill. ([City of Minneapolis: Special Cleanups](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/garbage/special-cleanups/))

**Donate first:** Twin Cities Habitat for Humanity ReStore takes furniture and appliances at 2700 Minnehaha Avenue and offers a free pickup for large or multiple donations; it does not take mattresses. ([Twin Cities Habitat for Humanity ReStore](https://restore.tchabitat.org/)) Bridging, a furniture bank, takes donations free at its Bloomington, Roseville and Plymouth warehouses, or picks up across most of the metro for $160 per booking. ([Bridging: Give Stuff](https://bridging.org/give-stuff/))

### Outside City Limits?

This guide covers the **City of Minneapolis** only, and the South Transfer Station is for City customers only. If your building uses dumpsters or carts from a private hauler, including many apartments, condos and HOA communities, ask your property manager or HOA first, and use Hennepin County's [Green Disposal Guide](https://www.hennepin.us/green-disposal-guide) to find where each item goes. **Saint Paul** and the suburbs each have their own hauler rules, so check your city's website. The Hennepin County drop-off facilities in Bloomington and Brooklyn Park serve Hennepin County households, and take household hazardous waste from Hennepin, Anoka, Carver, Dakota, Ramsey, Scott and Washington County households.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Curbside:** Air conditioners, refrigerators, freezers, microwaves, ovens, dishwashers, dryers and washing machines go out on your **recycling week**, labeled "For Solid Waste," as part of your two large items. **Remove the doors** from refrigerators and freezers. City staff take appliances apart and sell the metal for recycling. ([City of Minneapolis: Large Item Collection](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/large-items/))

**Drop-off:** Appliances also count as large recyclable items at the South Transfer Station (above). Hennepin County's drop-off facilities take large appliances for **$15 each**.

**Freon / refrigerants:** Never cut the lines or release refrigerant yourself; the US EPA says the final disposer in the disposal chain is responsible for making sure refrigerant is recovered. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Curbside:** TVs, computers, computer monitors and accessories (speakers, keyboards, mice) go out on your **recycling week**, labeled "For Solid Waste," as part of your two large items. They're trucked out for recycling. ([City of Minneapolis: Large Item Collection](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/large-items/))

**Drop-off:** Hennepin County charges **$10 each** for TVs, computer monitors and laptops. Computer peripherals, audio and video equipment, phones and rechargeable products are accepted at no charge, up to five of the same type a year.

**Batteries:** Single-use alkaline batteries (AA, AAA and similar) can go in your garbage cart. **Rechargeable batteries** are a fire hazard and are banned from all carts: tape the terminals or bag each one, and bring them to a Hennepin County drop-off facility or a retail drop-off site (see Other options at the bottom of this page). ([City of Minneapolis: Accepted Garbage](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/garbage-recycling-cleanup/garbage/accepted-garbage/))

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**Never** put paint, motor oil, oil filters or chemicals in your carts; the City won't pick them up, and the South Transfer Station doesn't take them either. Hennepin County takes them at its two drop-off facilities: ([Hennepin County: Drop-Off Facilities](https://www.hennepin.us/residents/recycling-hazardous-waste/drop-off-facilities))

- **Bloomington:** 1400 West 96th Street. **Brooklyn Park:** 8100 Jefferson Highway.
- **Hours:** Tuesday to Saturday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Closed Sunday and Monday and on holidays, including **Thanksgiving Day** and **Christmas Day**.
- **Free:** paint, stain, varnish and solvents, pesticides, aerosols, pool chemicals, fluorescent bulbs (up to 25 a year), mercury thermometers and thermostats, motor oil and filters, automotive fluids, fuels, and all types of batteries.
- **Bring** proof of residency, such as a driver's license or utility bill. Use sealed, labeled containers of 5 gallons or less, packed upright in a plastic-lined box, and stay in your vehicle unless staff direct you.
- **Not accepted:** furniture, carpet, construction or remodeling materials, medicines, ammunition, asbestos and business hazardous waste.
- **Questions:** call 612-348-3777 or check the Green Disposal Guide.

*Rules, hours and fees change. Confirm on the linked official page or with Solid Waste & Recycling at 612-673-2917 before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
