# Junk Disposal in Atlanta, GA: Mattresses, Couches, Furniture, Appliances, TVs & Electronics, Paint & Hazardous Waste

Here's how to legally get rid of bulky junk in Atlanta, Georgia, item by item. If you live in a **single-family home** served by the City of Atlanta, you get **12 free scheduled bulk collections a year** for furniture, mattresses and appliances, picked up on your regular collection day. **Apartment renters** usually can't use City bulk pickup, so see the note below. **Electronics, paint and chemicals** aren't taken in bulk pickup; the City sends residents to the nonprofit CHaRM drop-off center. Every option links to the official page we checked. Where we couldn't verify something, we say so. Check the linked page or call ATL311 (**404-546-0311**) before you set anything out.

## Quick Answer by Item

| Item | Best Verified Option |
|---|---|
| Mattress | Free scheduled City bulk collection (single-family homes), or CHaRM Atlanta drop-off for a fee |
| Couch / furniture | Free scheduled City bulk collection, up to 12 a year |
| Fridge / AC (freon) | Free scheduled City bulk collection (appliances are approved), or CHaRM Atlanta for a fee |
| TV / computer | Not taken in bulk pickup; CHaRM Atlanta (TVs and monitors carry a fee, most other electronics are free) |
| Paint / chemicals | The City has no HHW program; CHaRM Atlanta takes paint and chemicals, with the first 20 pounds free for residents |

## Mattresses and Box Springs {#mattresses}

**Mattress short answer:** Mattresses and box springs are on the City's approved list for **bulk collection**. Single-family residents can schedule it with the Solid Waste Services Collection Tool, through @ATL311 on Facebook, X or Instagram, or by calling ATL311 at 404-546-0311. ([City of Atlanta: Bulk Waste Collection](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/public-works/bulk-waste-collection))

<!--LISTINGS-->

**Drop-off:** CHaRM Atlanta, run by the nonprofit Live Thrive, takes home mattresses and box springs by appointment. It's the City's named recycling partner for hard-to-recycle items. ([Live Thrive: Items CHaRM Accepts](https://www.livethrive.org/charm/items-we-accept/))

**Bed bugs:** We couldn't find an official City of Atlanta rule about wrapping or bagging a mattress with bed bugs. Ask ATL311 when you schedule your pickup.

## Couches and Furniture {#furniture}

**Bulk collection:** The City's Department of Public Works gives single-family residents **12 free scheduled bulk collections a year**. Since February 16, 2026, bulk is picked up on your regular weekly collection day, but it still must be scheduled. Approved items include household furniture (sofas, loveseats, chairs, dressers, chests of drawers, nightstands, dining sets), mattresses and box springs, household appliances, barbecue grills and bulky yard trimmings kept in a separate pile. ([City of Atlanta: Bulk Waste Collection](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/public-works/bulk-waste-collection))

1. **Schedule it.** Book at least one business day ahead with the Solid Waste Services Collection Tool or ATL311 (404-546-0311). Only the resident of the address can schedule it. ([ATL311: Bulk Collection Request](https://www.atl311.com/en-US/knowledgearticle/?code=KB0011524))
2. **Set it out on time.** Put items at the curb in front of the property **no earlier than 7 p.m. the night before** your collection day. Setting out earlier can bring a citation.
3. **Place it right.** Keep bulk at least 6 feet from your garbage and recycling carts and yard trimmings. Don't block the street or sidewalk, and don't stack items under low wires or next to mailboxes, fences, utility meters or fire hydrants.
4. **Watch the size.** One collection is up to 8 cubic yards, about a small room of furniture. Anything over that counts as another collection.
5. **Cancel in time.** Cancel by 3 p.m. the day before. If you scheduled a pickup and nothing is out front on the day, it still counts as one of your 12.

**Not accepted:** building and construction materials, tires and auto parts, electronics (computers, printers and TVs), household hazardous waste (oils, fuels, paints, solvents), explosives or ammunition, metal drums, pressurized gas cylinders, and debris left by contractors. Bulk isn't collected for evictions or vacant properties.

**Over 12 a year:** The City charges a fee for each collection beyond 12. Its own pages currently list different amounts ($343.10 on the [City of Atlanta: Office of Solid Waste Services](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/public-works/office-of-solid-waste-services/) page and $336.38 in the ATL311 article), so confirm the current fee with ATL311 before you book.

### Outside City Limits?

**Apartments and renters:** City bulk collection is a service for **single-family** residential customers, and the City says it isn't available for materials from rental properties. If you rent, especially in an apartment building, ask your landlord or property manager how bulky items are handled. Townhome communities served by the City can schedule bulk collection and should add the gate code in the request comments. Renters without a truck can also book paid junk removal.

**Neighboring areas:** Many places with an Atlanta mailing address are outside City limits. **Unincorporated DeKalb County** and the cities of Decatur, Brookhaven, Dunwoody, Tucker and others are served by DeKalb County Solid Waste Management, which has its own bulky item request and fee rules; call 404-294-2900. ([DeKalb County: Bulky Items and Special Collections](https://dekalbcountyga.gov/departments/public-works/sanitation/bulky-items-and-special-collections)) Other neighboring cities, such as Sandy Springs, South Fulton and East Point, run their own services, so check your own city's page. If you live in an HOA or gated community, check with your HOA or property manager first.

## Appliances (Fridges, Freezers, AC: Freon) {#appliances}

**Bulk collection:** Household appliances such as refrigerators, washers, dryers, ovens and stoves are on the City's approved bulk list, so they go out with a scheduled bulk collection like furniture. We couldn't find a City rule on whether refrigerant must be removed first or whether fridge doors must come off, so ask ATL311 when you schedule.

**Drop-off:** CHaRM Atlanta takes large appliances, including refrigerators, dehumidifiers and humidifiers, for a fee of $15 to $20 each. ([Live Thrive: Items CHaRM Accepts](https://www.livethrive.org/charm/items-we-accept/))

**Freon:** Never cut the lines or let the refrigerant out yourself. The US EPA says the final disposer of an appliance is responsible for recovering its refrigerant. ([US EPA: Appliance Disposal](https://www.epa.gov/section608/appliance-disposal))

## TVs, Computers and Electronics (E-Waste) {#electronics}

**Not in bulk pickup:** The City doesn't collect electronics such as computers, printers and televisions in bulk collection, and says hard-to-recycle items shouldn't go in your recycling or garbage carts. ([City of Atlanta: Recycling Programs and Events](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/public-works/recycling-program))

**CHaRM Atlanta:** CHaRM takes any item that plugs in or charges. For residents, **TVs and monitors are $25 each** and all other electronics have no fee. Rear-projection TVs aren't accepted. Book an appointment first and bring your appointment QR code. ([Live Thrive: Items CHaRM Accepts](https://www.livethrive.org/charm/items-we-accept/))

**Atlanta Recycles Day:** On the third Saturday of each month from February through December, the Department of Public Works hosts Atlanta Recycles Day at Greenbriar Mall, where residents can recycle small electronics, tires and sensitive documents. The City's page doesn't list hours or which electronics count as small, so check before you go.

## Paint and Household Hazardous Waste {#hazardous}

**No City HHW program:** The City of Atlanta doesn't have a collection program for household hazardous waste and points residents to its recycling partner, CHaRM. ([City of Atlanta: Household Hazardous Waste Disposal](https://www.atlantaga.gov/government/departments/public-works/office-of-solid-waste-services/household-hazardous-waste-disposal))

**Latex paint:** The City says latex paint isn't considered hazardous but must be dried out before disposal. Leave the lid off so the paint dries, then put the can in the garbage with the lid off. Only empty cans or cans with solidified paint can go in your garbage cart. **Oil-based paint:** never put liquid paint in the garbage. The City says to mix in kitty litter or sawdust to solidify it first.

**CHaRM Atlanta:** CHaRM takes latex, oil-based and aerosol paint plus household chemicals such as pesticides, pool chemicals, drain cleaner, motor oil and gasoline. Chemicals must be in their original containers, and containers of 5 gallons or more aren't accepted. ([Live Thrive: CHaRM](https://livethrive.org/charm/))

- **Fees for residents:** Paint and chemicals are weighed together. The first 20 pounds are free. Above that, CHaRM charges $0.70 a pound up to 100 pounds total, $0.75 a pound from 101 to 199 pounds, and $0.80 a pound for 200 pounds or more.
- **Not accepted:** propane tanks of any size, alkaline batteries and car batteries.
- **Closures:** CHaRM lists 2026 closures on November 25 (closing early), November 26 to 28, December 24 to 28 and December 31, plus January 1.

Questions? Call **ATL311** at **404-546-0311** (or 3-1-1 inside the city), CHaRM at **404-600-6386**, or DeKalb County Solid Waste Management at **404-294-2900**.

*Rules, fees and hours change. Confirm on the linked official page or with the City before you set anything out or drive anywhere.*
